### 7e. INDI Inner Loop Dashboard (`_indi_dashboard.png`)

**What this figure is**: A 3×3 overview of the INDI inner loop during the full flight. Each column is one body axis (X=roll, Y=pitch, Z=yaw). Source: `analyze_indi.py`.

![](figure8_mode1_kt0.05_2026-06-20_14-49-59_indi_dashboard.png)

**Row 0 — Attitude (roll / pitch / yaw)** — Full-flight attitude time series from the EKF. Roll and pitch stay within the expected ±30° figure-8 envelope; no oscillation is visible, confirming the Routh stability margin (ζ=1.34) is adequate. The title shows RMS and peak values over the flight window.

**Row 1 — INDI Commanded Torque (τ_x / τ_y / τ_z)** — The torque output of the INDI inner loop for each axis, in N·m. This is what the INDI law sends to the motor mixer every 500 Hz control cycle. The title shows the RMS over the valid flight window and the saturation percentage (how often tau hit the estimated motor torque limit).
- The first ~2.7 s are shaded orange: this is a "stale tau" period where tau holds the value from the previous power-on and is not yet under active INDI control. All statistics exclude this period.
- RMS values (≈1.4 / 0.8 / 0.2 mN·m for roll/pitch/yaw) are physically plausible for a 36 g drone.
- Saturation = 0% — the INDI torque is well within motor capability; gains can be raised further.
- Peaks at the crossing-point of the figure-8 correspond to the highest angular acceleration demand. tau_z (yaw) is much smaller than tau_x/y as expected (yaw demands during a figure-8 are minimal).

**Row 2 — Angular Acceleration Filter (alp_raw vs alp)** — Angular acceleration is computed by finite-differencing the gyro signal and then low-pass filtered at fc_bw=70 Hz. This row compares the raw (pre-filter) and filtered signals over the hover calibration window. The title shows noise reduction in dB and filter lag in ms.
- `alp_raw` and `alp` overlap almost perfectly because the 20 Hz log rate cannot show the high-frequency noise that exists at the 500 Hz control rate — the filter IS attenuating above 70 Hz, it is simply invisible in a 20 Hz log.
- The dB noise reduction number comes from comparing the power spectral density of alp_raw vs alp over the hover window.
- Lag ≈ 2–3 ms at 70 Hz — negligible compared to the 40–50 ms motor response lag.

### 7f. INDI Alignment Panel (`_indi_panel.png`)

**What this figure is**: A direct comparison of the torque the INDI law commanded (τ_cmd, orange) vs what the drone actually produced as measured via angular acceleration (J·α_meas, green). Tests the core INDI assumption that τ_prev ≈ J·α_meas. Source: `analyze_indi.py`.

![](figure8_mode1_kt0.05_2026-06-20_14-49-59_indi_panel.png)

**Rows 0–2 — τ_cmd vs J·α_meas for X (roll) / Y (pitch) / Z (yaw)**
- Orange line = τ_cmd: torque commanded by INDI in the previous timestep (τ_prev).
- Green line = J·α_meas: moment-of-inertia times the measured angular acceleration this timestep — what the drone actually did.
- Shaded area = the difference (τ_cmd − J·α_meas), i.e. the INDI residual error.
- **Lag** (title): the orange line leads the green by ~40–50 ms. This is the motor spin-up delay — motors take 40–50 ms to reach the commanded RPM, so angular acceleration lags the torque command by this amount. This is the fundamental hardware ceiling: reducing it would require DSHOT or brushless upgrade.
- **r** (title): cross-correlation coefficient. r≈0.9 means the shapes match well despite the lag.
- **RMS(delta)** (title): residual INDI error in mN·m. Small relative to signal amplitude — INDI is effectively compensating for disturbances despite the motor lag.
- Note: the absolute scale difference between orange and green is not physically meaningful (τ is in firmware units while J·α is in N·m); lag and RMS delta are shape-based and scale-independent.

**Row 3 — KT Sanity Check (thrust vs weight)** — RPM-derived total thrust (Σkt·RPM²) compared against the drone's weight (36.4 g × 9.81 = 357 mN). At hover, total thrust should equal weight. The title reports the percentage deviation and flags if the kt calibration is outside ±15%. Any significant error here would undermine INDI's torque model since tau depends on the per-motor kt values.

### 7g. Linear Acceleration Comparison (`_indi_accel.png`)

**What this figure is**: World-frame linear acceleration from two independent sources — the IMU (accelerometer rotated to world frame, gravity removed) vs the EKF (numerical derivative of velocity). Agreement validates that the IMU axis alignment and the EKF state are consistent. Source: `analyze_indi.py`.

![](figure8_mode1_kt0.05_2026-06-20_14-49-59_indi_accel.png)

**X (forward)** — RMS gap = 0.43 m/s²: a_imu and a_ekf agree well in both sign and shape. Residual gap is mostly high-frequency IMU noise and EKF smoothing (the Kalman filter inherently low-passes the velocity estimate). Good X agreement confirms the drone's forward acceleration is correctly captured.

**Y (left)** — RMS gap = 0.46 m/s²: similarly good agreement, slightly higher than X because Y demands more asymmetric acceleration at the lobe tips.

**Z (up)** — RMS gap = 0.15 m/s²: the tightest of the three axes, as expected. Z acceleration is dominated by the slowly varying thrust magnitude with little body-rotation noise. Near-zero mean gap confirms gravity subtraction (a_z_body − 1 g) is correctly normalised.

## 8. Next Steps

1. **Fix USD logging and record new data with SD card** — current logging relies on the radio link which limits rate and introduces gaps. SD card logging gives clean high-rate data for all signals.

2. **Redo filter sweep to confirm noise is properly filtered** — repeat the fc_bw sweep with SD card data to verify that the 70 Hz Butterworth is actually attenuating gyro differentiation noise at 500 Hz control rate (invisible in the current 20 Hz log).

3. **Switch to brushless Crazyflie and redo full tuning session** — higher thrust-to-weight ratio reduces motor saturation at the crossing-point turns; repeat the full filter sweep, attitude gains sweep, and position gains sweep on the new platform.

4. **Fix filter order in the signal chain** — currently the gyro data arriving from the sensor is already pre-filtered onboard; our Butterworth is applied on top of that, resulting in an uncontrolled combined filter response. The correct approach is to take raw gyro data and apply two explicit filters on our side with known cutoffs, giving full control over the filtering chain.
