### 7e. INDI Inner Loop Dashboard (`_indi_dashboard.png`)

**What this figure is**: A 3×3 overview of the INDI inner loop during the full flight. Each column is one body axis (X=roll, Y=pitch, Z=yaw). Source: `analyze_indi.py`.

![](figure8_mode1_kt0.05_2026-06-20_14-49-59_indi_dashboard.png)

**Row 0 — Attitude (roll / pitch / yaw)** — Full-flight attitude time series from the EKF. Roll and pitch stay within the expected ±30° figure-8 envelope; no oscillation is visible, confirming the Routh stability margin (ζ=1.34) is adequate. The title shows RMS and peak values over the flight window.

**Row 1 — INDI Commanded Torque (τ_x / τ_y / τ_z)** — The torque output of the INDI inner loop for each axis, in N·m. This is what the INDI law sends to the motor mixer every 500 Hz control cycle. The title shows the RMS over the valid flight window and the saturation percentage (how often tau hit the estimated motor torque limit).
- The first ~2.7 s are shaded orange: this is a "stale tau" period where tau holds the value from the previous power-on and is not yet under active INDI control. All statistics exclude this period.
- RMS values (≈1.4 / 0.8 / 0.2 mN·m for roll/pitch/yaw) are physically plausible for a 36 g drone.
- Saturation = 0% — the INDI torque is well within motor capability; gains can be raised further.
- Peaks at the crossing-point of the figure-8 correspond to the highest angular acceleration demand. tau_z (yaw) is much smaller than tau_x/y as expected (yaw demands during a figure-8 are minimal).

**Row 2 — Angular Acceleration Filter (alp_raw vs alp)** — Angular acceleration is computed by finite-differencing the gyro signal and then low-pass filtered at fc_bw=70 Hz. This row compares the raw (pre-filter) and filtered signals over the hover calibration window. The title shows noise reduction in dB and filter lag in ms.
- `alp_raw` and `alp` overlap almost perfectly because the 20 Hz log rate cannot show the high-frequency noise that exists at the 500 Hz control rate — the filter IS attenuating above 70 Hz, it is simply invisible in a 20 Hz log.
- The dB noise reduction number comes from comparing the power spectral density of alp_raw vs alp over the hover window.
- Lag ≈ 2–3 ms at 70 Hz — negligible compared to the 40–50 ms motor response lag.

### 7f. INDI Alignment Panel (`_indi_panel.png`)

**What this figure is**: A direct comparison of the torque the INDI law commanded (τ_cmd, orange) vs what the drone actually produced as measured via angular acceleration (J·α_meas, green). Tests the core INDI assumption that τ_prev ≈ J·α_meas. Source: `analyze_indi.py`.

![](figure8_mode1_kt0.05_2026-06-20_14-49-59_indi_panel.png)

**Rows 0–2 — τ_cmd vs J·α_meas for X (roll) / Y (pitch) / Z (yaw)**
- Orange line = τ_cmd: torque commanded by INDI in the previous timestep (τ_prev).
- Green line = J·α_meas: moment-of-inertia times the measured angular acceleration this timestep — what the drone actually did.
- Shaded area = the difference (τ_cmd − J·α_meas), i.e. the INDI residual error.
- **Lag** (title): the orange line leads the green by ~40–50 ms. This is the motor spin-up delay — motors take 40–50 ms to reach the commanded RPM, so angular acceleration lags the torque command by this amount. This is the fundamental hardware ceiling: reducing it would require DSHOT or brushless upgrade.
- **r** (title): cross-correlation coefficient. r≈0.9 means the shapes match well despite the lag.
- **RMS(delta)** (title): residual INDI error in mN·m. Small relative to signal amplitude — INDI is effectively compensating for disturbances despite the motor lag.
- Note: the absolute scale difference between orange and green is not physically meaningful (τ is in firmware units while J·α is in N·m); lag and RMS delta are shape-based and scale-independent.

**Row 3 — KT Sanity Check (thrust vs weight)** — RPM-derived total thrust (Σkt·RPM²) compared against the drone's weight (36.4 g × 9.81 = 357 mN). At hover, total thrust should equal weight. The title reports the percentage deviation and flags if the kt calibration is outside ±15%. Any significant error here would undermine INDI's torque model since tau depends on the per-motor kt values.

### 7g. Linear Acceleration Comparison (`_indi_accel.png`)

**What this figure is**: World-frame linear acceleration from two independent sources — the IMU (accelerometer rotated to world frame, gravity removed) vs the EKF (numerical derivative of velocity). Agreement validates that the IMU axis alignment and the EKF state are consistent. Source: `analyze_indi.py`.

![](figure8_mode1_kt0.05_2026-06-20_14-49-59_indi_accel.png)

**X (forward)** — RMS gap = 0.43 m/s²: a_imu and a_ekf agree well in both sign and shape. Residual gap is mostly high-frequency IMU noise and EKF smoothing (the Kalman filter inherently low-passes the velocity estimate). Good X agreement confirms the drone's forward acceleration is correctly captured.

**Y (left)** — RMS gap = 0.46 m/s²: similarly good agreement, slightly higher than X because Y demands more asymmetric acceleration at the lobe tips.

**Z (up)** — RMS gap = 0.15 m/s²: the tightest of the three axes, as expected. Z acceleration is dominated by the slowly varying thrust magnitude with little body-rotation noise. Near-zero mean gap confirms gravity subtraction (a_z_body − 1 g) is correctly normalised.

## 8. Next Steps

1. **Fix USD logging and record new data with SD card** — current logging relies on the radio link which limits rate and introduces gaps. SD card logging gives clean high-rate data for all signals.

2. **Redo filter sweep to confirm noise is properly filtered** — repeat the fc_bw sweep with SD card data to verify that the 70 Hz Butterworth is actually attenuating gyro differentiation noise at 500 Hz control rate (invisible in the current 20 Hz log).

3. **Switch to brushless Crazyflie and redo full tuning session** — higher thrust-to-weight ratio reduces motor saturation at the crossing-point turns; repeat the full filter sweep, attitude gains sweep, and position gains sweep on the new platform.

4. **Fix filter order in the signal chain** — currently the gyro data arriving from the sensor is already pre-filtered onboard; our Butterworth is applied on top of that, resulting in an uncontrolled combined filter response. The correct approach is to take raw gyro data and apply two explicit filters on our side with known cutoffs, giving full control over the filtering chain.

## v2-improvements Branch — Round 1: Tilt Gravity Compensation (2026-07-01)

**Branch:** `v2-improvements` | **Drone:** second drone (different RPM deck + motors)
**Change:** `ENABLE_TILT_GRAVITY_COMP = true` — replaces `GRAVITY` constant with `gz_comp = GRAVITY / R[2][2].max(0.5)` in the position loop force demand. ### Analysis

**Stable flights (RPM spread ≤5%, gyro X std ≤105 °/s) — 5 flights:**
- XY RMSE: **2.4–3.1 cm** vs 4.2–4.3 cm baseline → **30–40% improvement**
- t_shift flipped: +180 ms → −90 to −100 ms. Previously lagging, now slightly ahead. Tilt compensation is adding thrust sooner and more aggressively, slightly over-driving the trajectory. Not a problem at this amplitude.
- Geometric RMSE: 1.5–2.0 cm → shape-tracking is excellent

**Crashes / degraded (RPM spread >6%) — 6 flights:**
- Crash signature is identical to June 30 sticker-failure sessions: RPM spread climbs (6→8→10→16%), gyro std spikes (150→280 °/s), RMSE explodes
- Root cause: **sticker delamination on the RPM deck of the second drone**, same problem as the first drone. Not caused by the tilt compensation firmware change.
- The crash rate increased across the session (last 5 flights: 3 crashes vs 1 crash in first 6), suggesting stickers progressively detached under vibration during the session.

### Verdict

**Round 1 (tilt gravity compensation): IMPROVEMENT CONFIRMED** on stable flights, **but sticker reliability blocks further testing.**

- On flights where RPM readings were healthy: tilt compensation consistently delivers 2.4–3.1 cm RMSE (best 2.4 cm)
- The crashes are hardware (stickers), not firmware — confirmed by RPM spread being the leading indicator in every case
- **Before Round 2**: reapply reflective stickers on the second drone (all 4 motors, outer edge, pressed firmly). Once stickers are stable, Round 1 result is expected to be 2.4–3.1 cm consistently.

#### Methodology note — RPM must be measured in the steady window only

Raw RPM logs always contain zeros before takeoff (logging starts before motors spin) and large
transient spread during the 1 s spin-up ramp. Counting those as "dropouts" is an artifact. All RPM
metrics below are computed on the **airborne steady window only**: rows where the 4-motor mean
> 15 000 RPM, excluding the first 1 s after becoming airborne. A **genuine mid-flight dropout** is
defined as *a motor reading < 8 000 RPM while a sibling reads > 18 000 RPM* (i.e. one motor died
while the drone was clearly flying).

#### Two remaining hypotheses, and the decisive test

Both survivors are intermittent and consistent with 20:03:
- **H1 — mocap/EKF velocity noise**: position jitter differentiated into ±1 m/s velocity spikes →

**Decisive test (onset time-series):** on an unstable flight, does mocap-derived velocity go noisy

**Verdict — the physical signal leads, the estimate follows:**

- At the **first second airborne**, the unstable flight's raw gyro is already **1.8× higher**
  (42.4 vs 23.8 °/s) — while the mocap velocity estimate is **identical** (0.23 = 0.23 m/s).
- The gyro then rings up **exponentially** (42 → 200 °/s over 7 s) and saturates into a **limit
  cycle** at ~200 °/s. `vx` noise grows *behind* it as a consequence of the drone physically moving.
- The stable flight **damps** everything (gyro 24 → 10, roll 3 → 1).

This is the signature of a **divergent closed-loop oscillation (limit cycle)**, not mocap velocity
noise being fed forward. If H1 (estimate noise driving phantoms) were the mechanism, `vx` would be
elevated *at onset* — it is not; it starts identical and lags. **H1 in its simple form is rejected.**

#### Refined root cause

The loop is **conditionally unstable**: identical gains/firmware damp on 20:03 but diverge on 19:59.
For a fixed-gain loop to flip stability flight-to-flight, its **stability margin** must change — i.e.
**loop delay or plant gain varied between flights**. The most plausible intermittent variable is the
**mocap→drone external-pose stream** (latency/jitter from the observed flickering marker and frame
drops). Increased effective delay eats phase margin; the **already-underdamped KP=40 position loop
(ζ≈0.26)** then tips into the divergent oscillation seen above. This unifies the evidence:

- **Trigger**: intermittent mocap latency/quality (flickering marker → frame drops) — explains the
  flight-to-flight intermittency and the 20:03 stable window.
- **Amplifier**: KP=40 underdamped loop — explains why D2@KP40 rings up violently (roll → 34°) while
  D1@KP28 only degraded (roll ~10°) under similar conditions.
- **Symptoms** (downstream): elevated motor spread, elevated hover RPM, growing `vx` noise.

**Conclusion:** the v2 firmware did not cause the instability, and RPM dropout is refuted. The
late-session instability is a **conditional control-loop divergence** triggered by intermittent
motion-capture pose latency/quality and amplified by the underdamped KP=40 gain. Recommended fixes,
in order: (1) fix the flickering mocap marker / verify extpose stream health; (2) reduce KP toward 28
to restore phase margin; (3) add velocity-feedback filtering. RPM-deck contact should still be
serviced for the two genuine INDI dropout flights (19:16, 19:34), but it is not the primary driver.

### Crash-cause summary (both INDI and geometric)

Both controllers **share the same outer position→attitude loop** (`g_kp_xy`/`g_kv_xy`, force =
`ff + KP·ep + KV·ev + KI·i_ep`); they differ only in the inner attitude-torque law. So a marginal
outer loop appears in **both** modes, and both crash types share one fingerprint: roll rings up
exponentially into a limit cycle, gyro climbs to ~200 °/s, **no** mid-flight RPM dropout, physical
gyro leads the estimate.

- **It is not random sensor noise.** It is a loop with a small stability margin that an intermittent
  disturbance occasionally tips over. Noise is the *trigger*; the underdamped KP=40 tuning is the
  *reason a trigger becomes a crash instead of a wobble*.
- **Causal chain:** underdamped high-gain loop (always present, the real weakness) + intermittent
  mocap latency/quality dip (the trigger) → phase margin lost → divergent oscillation → motor
  saturation → crash.
- **Why base rates differ:** geometric hover exposes the outer loop fully (almost always unstable at
  KP=40); INDI figure-8's inner loop + continuous motion mask it most of the time (mostly DEGRADED
  ~10°, occasional crash) — same weakness, different exposure.
- **Two levers remove it:** (1) remove the trigger — fix the mocap marker / verify extpose stream;
  (2) widen the margin — filter velocity + raise damping (see Round 2 below) so the same hiccup
  produces a bump, not a divergence.

## v2-improvements — Round 2 (planned): Robustness / margin-widening fixes

**Goal:** make the drone *shrug off* an intermittent mocap latency/quality dip instead of diverging,
so KP can stay at 40 (where INDI tracks better) without crashing. Same compile-time flag pattern as
Round 1 — one flag at a time, all default `false` (⇒ binary identical to stable). **stable-jun30-working
stays untouched; all changes land on `v2-improvements` only.**

### What "widen the margin" means (first principles)

The XY position loop is a mass with a spring (KP) and damper (KV). Damping ratio
`ζ = KV / (2·√KP)` and bandwidth `ωₙ = √KP`:

| Config | ωₙ (rad/s) | ζ | Delay tolerance |
|--------|-----------|-----|-----------------|
| KP=28, KV=3.0 | 5.29 | 0.28 | higher (lower BW) — why 28 survived |
| **KP=40, KV=3.3** | 6.32 | **0.26** | **lower — fragile to mocap latency** |
| KP=40, KV≈9 (target) | 6.32 | **0.71** | high — well-damped, delay-tolerant |

**Phase margin** = how much extra loop delay the loop tolerates before it oscillates. Underdamped
(low ζ) + high bandwidth (high KP) = small phase margin = small tolerable delay. A mocap latency
spike adds delay; when it exceeds the margin, the loop diverges. Widening the margin = **add damping
(raise KV toward ζ≈0.7)** and/or **lower KP**. But raising KV multiplies velocity-estimate noise
(`KV·ev`), so damping alone injects HF noise — which is why it must be paired with a **velocity
low-pass filter**. Filter first, then damping can do its job.

### Fixes (v2-improvements `firmware_app/src/lib.rs`)

All four flags default `false` ⇒ each helper returns its input unchanged ⇒ binary identical to stable.

**Filtering-consistency rule** (why cutoffs differ): filter every **rate/derivative feedback term
that multiplies a damping gain**, with a cutoff **matched to that loop's bandwidth** — attitude/α
signals feed the fast inner loop → high cutoff (`FC_BW_HZ`, `GYRO_FB_FC_HZ`≈50 Hz); velocity feeds
the slow position loop → low cutoff (10 Hz). **Do NOT** filter the primary position measurement
(`KP·ep` — least noisy, dominant term, lag hurts most) or the gyroscopic feedforward (ω×Jω).

### Full signal-filtering audit — every filterable parameter in both loops

**Position (outer) loop**

| # | Signal | Filtered? | How | Why / why not |
|---|--------|-----------|-----|---------------|
| 1 | Position `pos` → `ep` (KP·ep) | ❌ No | — | Primary measurement, least noisy (not differentiated) and dominant term. Lag on the biggest gain = worst phase-margin cost. Rely on EKF smoothing. |
| 2 | Velocity `vel` → `ev` (KV·ev) | ✅ Yes | Butterworth **10 Hz** (`ENABLE_VEL_FB_FILTER`) + clamp ±0.6 (`ENABLE_POSE_GLITCH_GUARD`) | Differentiated position → HF noise, multiplied by damping gain KV we want to raise. Filter lets KV rise without injecting noise. Low cutoff = slow loop. |
| 3 | Position integral `i_ep` (KI·i_ep) | ➖ Inherent | integration + `KI_LIMIT` clamp | Integration is itself a low-pass; clamp prevents wind-up. Gated by `ENABLE_POSITION_INTEGRAL`. |
| 4 | Accelerometer `acc_body` → a_indi | ✅ Yes | Butterworth `FC_BW_HZ` (`ENABLE_ACC_PREFILTER`) | Noisy accel used in position-INDI `a_meas−a_model`; noise would reach the force command. Cutoff = INDI bandwidth. |
| 5 | Feedforward `pd, vd, ad` | ❌ No | — | From the trajectory polynomial — smooth by construction; filtering only adds lag. |

**Attitude (inner) loop**

| # | Signal | Filtered? | How | Why / why not |
|---|--------|-----------|-----|---------------|
| 6 | Attitude `r` → `er` (KR·er) | ❌ No | — | Primary attitude, already fused by the EKF; dominant term. Lag on biggest gain costs most; SO(3) filtering non-trivial. |
| 7 | Attitude integral `i_error_att` | ➖ Inherent | integration | Integration is inherently low-pass. Gated by `ENABLE_ATTITUDE_INTEGRAL`. |
| 8 | Gyro `ω` in `e_omega` (KW damping) | ✅ Yes | Butterworth **50 Hz** (`ENABLE_GYRO_FB_FILTER`) | Noisy MEMS gyro × damping gain KW → torque noise. High cutoff = fast loop, cut noise without lag at crossover. (Fix C.) |
| 9 | Gyro `ω` in gyroscopic FF `ω×Jω` | ❌ No | — | Feedforward, not feedback — filtering would delay the compensation it cancels. |
| 10 | Gyro `ω` → finite-diff → `alpha_meas` (INDI) | ✅✅ Yes | Butterworth `FC_BW_HZ` **+ IIR** | Differentiating gyro massively amplifies HF noise — noisiest operation → heaviest (two-stage) filtering. Core INDI, always on. |
| 11 | `alpha_ref` (INDI reference α) | ✅ Yes | Butterworth `FC_BW_HZ` (`bw_ref`) | Phase alignment, not noise: must pass the same filter as `alpha_meas` for matched delay. Always on. |
| 12 | Per-motor RPM → `tau_current` / a_model | ⚠️ Guarded, not filtered | outlier hold (`ENABLE_RPM_GUARD`) | Fault is discrete dropouts, not HF noise. A low-pass would smear a dropout; hold-last-valid is the right tool (Fix A). |

**Unifying rule:** filter every rate/derivative *feedback* term that multiplies a damping gain, cutoff
matched to that loop's bandwidth; leave primary measurements, feedforwards, and already-integrated
signals raw; guard (don't filter) discrete faults. After Fixes 5/C + imp-3, everything that should be
filtered is.

### Analysis

**Step 0 verdict: ✅ CONFIRMED STABLE** — all flights successful except the INDI no-takeoff
(operator issue, not firmware). Step 0 establishes the baseline before any improvements are enabled.

**Controller comparison at KP=28 (today) vs KP=40 (Section 4):**

| Controller | KP | Geom RMSE (today, n=2) | Geom RMSE (Sec 4, n=10) |
|------------|----|------------------------|--------------------------|
| Geometric | 28 | 3.6 / 4.7 cm (avg **4.15 cm**) | 6.14 cm avg |
| fullINDI | 28 | 2.9 / 3.0 cm (avg **2.95 cm**) | 3.87 cm avg (at KP=40) |

Geometric at KP=28 tracks significantly better today than Section 4 at KP=40 (4.15 vs 6.14 cm).
This is counterintuitive since higher KP should improve tracking — the gap is largely explained by
the more aggressive Section 4 being flown on a *different drone* (D2 with sticker issues inflating
RMSE variance) and mocap quality on the day. Both data points are only n=2; more flights needed
before drawing strong conclusions.

INDI at KP=28 (2.95 cm avg) is comparable to INDI at KP=40 (3.87 cm avg). INDI's tracking
advantage comes more from the inner-loop torque model than from a high position-loop KP. The outer
loop gain sweep (Section 3) showed KP=40 was better (4.5 vs 5.1 cm), but those were single flights;
today's spread (2.9–4.7 cm) overlaps substantially.

**INDI advantage over geometric held:** 2.95 cm vs 4.15 cm geometric at the same KP=28/KV=3.0 =
**~29% improvement** — consistent direction with the 37% in Section 4.

**t_shift (phase lag):** Geometric +190 ms / −120 ms (sign inconsistency = single-lap jitter).
INDI consistently +160 ms, same as Section 4. Lag is dominated by the motor response time
(~40–50 ms per INDI panel analysis) and the 25 Hz HLC update rate. Remaining lag is structural.

**RPM spread:** Geo ~950 (higher — controller fighting), INDI ~800 (lower — inner loop closing
faster). No dropouts in steady window. RPM deck sticker is holding.

**Next step (Step 1):** raise YAML KP=40/KV=3.3 (no reflash) and confirm whether the instability
from July 1 reappears. If it does, the underdamped-loop / mocap-trigger hypothesis is confirmed.

### Key findings

1. **Speed limit confirmed at kt=0.15** (5.32 s/lap, ~1.08× figure-8 frequency): stable with Geom RMSE 3.4–4.1 cm. kt=0.20 caused genuine tracking failure (RMSE 168–175 cm) on both attempts.
2. **RMSE degrades gracefully with speed**: 2.0 cm → 3.0 cm → 3.7 cm as kt goes 0.05 → 0.10 → 0.15. Expected — faster trajectory leaves less time for attitude loop to respond.
3. **kt=0.10 is the practical sweet spot**: 14% faster lap than kt=0.05, RMSE +50% (still <5 cm), no genuine breakdown.
4. **RPM deck sticker delamination** caused 5 of 7 crashes (rpm_spread > 16,000 vs normal 5,000–11,000). Worsened through session after kt=0.20 hard crashes. Physical repair required before further INDI flights.
5. **Planning theoretical limit**: feasibility check (max_thrust=0.55 N, max_omega=15 rad/s) gives kt≈0.40–0.45 as the CF2.1 ceiling. Practical controller limit is kt=0.15–0.20, well below the kinematic limit — controller bandwidth is the bottleneck, not thrust.
