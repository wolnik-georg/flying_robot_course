## 3. Learning rate (step 1c)

**What is stated.** The DiffCoTune paper does not state the learning rate (p. 4: only "Adam", and "identical design choices (learning rate and maximum epochs, K)" for the ablation). Adam's default is 1e-3 (Kingma & Ba 2015, "Adam: a method for stochastic optimization", recommended α = 0.001; the PyTorch default is also 1e-3). Optax's `adam` takes the learning rate as an argument; whether it has a default was **not checked here**. DiffTune (another paper) compared 0.1 / 0.01 / 0.001 with plain gradient descent on raw gains and used 0.1 on hardware with a ±10 % trust region and 0.01 in simulation; its values do not transfer to Adam on log-ratios. In our parameterisation φ = log(gain / default gain), Adam moves each φ by about lr per epoch when the gradient sign stays the same, so lr 0.01 is at most about 1 % per epoch and at most about 65 % (exp 0.5) in a 50-epoch inner loop.

**Probe** (S0, plant P1, paper variant: full-state task cost, p+v+q+w fit, paper stop rule, no noise, no trust region, forward gradient, 5 scenarios; 3 flown rounds each; the real cost after round 1 is the flight of θ₁):

| lr | real 3-D RMS after round 0 / 1 / 2 (cm) | fit epochs (rounds 0, 1, 2) | tune epochs | largest gain change to next set (rounds 0, 1) | crashes | floor flights per round |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1e-3 | 18.24 / 18.31 / 18.44 | 26, 2, 19 | 50, 50, 10 | 5.7 %, 6.1 % | 0 | 1, 1, 1 |
| 3e-3 | 18.24 / 17.67 / 17.57 | 29, 8, 6 | 50, 30, 17 | 16.0 %, 8.8 % | 0 | 1, 1, 1 |
| 1e-2 | 18.24 / 17.24 / 22.09 | 18, 2, 8 | 14, 41, 7 | 13.8 %, 45.7 % | 0 | 1, 1, 1 |

**Rule** (user): best real cost after round 1 with no crash or floor flight; within 0.3 cm take the smaller lr. Real cost after round 1: 18.31 / 17.67 / 17.24 cm for 1e-3 / 3e-3 / 1e-2 (the default gains give 18.24 cm). No probe had a crash. A1 is a floor flight at the default gains in this dry-run plant in every probe (so the floor criterion does not separate the rates). **Chosen: lr 0.01** (0.43 cm better than 3e-3). Note: at 1e-2 the flight of θ₂ was worse (22.09 cm, largest gain change 45.7 % between θ₁ and θ₂): without a trust region one update can move the gains far; see the safety note.

## 4. RPM delay from real data (step 1d)

* **Where 4.0502 ms comes from.** `simulator/frsim/data/calibration.json` (plant of record, frozen 10 Nov): `rpm_delay_ms` 4.0501500631407525, copied from `experiments/tuning/out/plant_calibration_joint_lag30_best_s0.json`. It is **one of the parameters fitted jointly** to 28 hardware targets (calibration objective 1.40) in the recalibration of 30 Oct 2026 with the motor lag fixed at 30 ms and the other seven parameters re-fitted (`docs/tuning_plant_p4_2026-10-29.md`, update of 30 Oct; before it the fit gave 17.6 ms for a lag of 22 ms). So 4.05 ms is a fitted value of the simulator, not a direct measurement.
* **What was measured on hardware.** (i) `docs/tuning_actuator_id_2026-10-29.md`: command-to-DShot-telemetry delay of the motors from 187 flights, median **6 ms** for S0, S2, S1, S3 and the NS2 / pilot groups, 4 ms for FBL (time constant 30 ms; log resolution 2 ms; the INDI flights fit worse, R² 0.76–0.83); range quoted "4–6 ms". (ii) `docs/43_RPM_Source_Quality.md` and `experiments/analysis/out/rpm_source_quality/`: DShot lags the optical deck by **0–2 ms** (median over 32 motor-rows from 8 flights; typical 0–5 ms; 2 ms grid at 500 Hz).
* **Which source and filtering the firmware uses.** `indi_gains.rpm_source` = 1 (DShot) since 2026-09-18, chosen for reliability not speed (the deck reported zero on two motors in two-drone flights); a spike guard in `rpm_get_all()` since 2026-09-29; the INDI part filters a_rpm, a_imu, τ_rpm and the angular acceleration with a 2nd-order Butterworth, 30 Hz (`s1_model.py`, from `controller_omar_indi.c`).
* **Consistency.** The model's `rpm_delay_ms` is the delay of the rpm signal the INDI sees against the true motor force; the measured 4–6 ms command-to-telemetry delay (with the 30 ms lag in front) is the same kind of quantity. 4.05 ms lies at the lower edge of the measured 4–6 ms. What is **not** measured: the optical deck's own delay, and the exact DShot processing delay inside the firmware (only the relative lag deck–DShot, 0–2 ms, is). The dry-run plant truth of 8 ms (P1RD) is an assumption of the plan, not data.
* **Proposal (the user decides):** 4.05 ms stays: it is consistent with the data (4–6 ms), and moving it to 6 ms (the median of the actuator identification) would change a value that was fitted jointly with the other plant parameters without refitting them.
