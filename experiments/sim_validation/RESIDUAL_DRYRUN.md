Collect → train → predict → compensate, entirely in simulation, through the same code paths the lab will use. **These are simulation numbers.** They say the pipeline works end to end; they say nothing about how well the method works on a real drone, where the residual is measured rather than generated by the same kind of model being fitted.

## 1. Does the model predict? (phase 3, `rnn.en=0`)

Prediction and measurement are independent here — the network is not touching the vehicle — so these numbers mean what they say.

| Drone | RMSE(a_res) | RMSE(pred − a_res) | reduction | corr | clamped |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cf231_active | 0.3410 | 0.0328 | +90.4% | 0.994 | 0.0% |
| cf_second | 0.1246 | 0.0301 | +75.9% | 0.967 | 0.0% |

All values m/s². *Reduction* is how much of the measured residual the prediction accounts for; a model that cannot beat predicting zero shows ≤ 0%.


## 2. Does compensation help? (4 compensate vs 1 collect)

Error in the *separation between the vehicles*: realised minus commanded. Downwash shows up as a **bias** — the lower vehicle sags, so the held gap closes — which is why the mean is the headline and the spread is secondary.

| Pair | Axis | quantity | 1 collect | 4 compensate | change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cf231_active–cf_second | x | mean error | 0.00 mm | 0.00 mm | baseline < 1 mm |
| cf231_active–cf_second | x | spread (std) | 1.65 mm | 1.14 mm | -31.1% |
| cf231_active–cf_second | y | mean error | 0.00 mm | 0.01 mm | baseline < 1 mm |
| cf231_active–cf_second | y | spread (std) | 0.00 mm | 0.30 mm | baseline < 1 mm |
| cf231_active–cf_second | z | mean error | 4.66 mm | 0.63 mm | -86.6% |
| cf231_active–cf_second | z | spread (std) | 11.74 mm | 3.22 mm | -72.6% |


## Verdict

**Pipeline works end to end.** The model accounts for 76% of the measured residual on the worst drone with the loop open, and the compensation phase ran with `rnn.en=1` without destabilising the vehicle. Turning it on took the steady separation error on cf231_active–cf_second z from 4.66 mm to 0.63 mm.
