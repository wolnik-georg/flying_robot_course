What the literature work actually changed

Reading the papers in full rather than from abstracts moved several claims. A headline 36 % improvement turned out to be lateral, not three-dimensional — the paper contradicts its own abstract. A 5 ms control loop cited as evidence of onboard feasibility runs off-board on a laptop. A result read as refuting force superposition in fact reports three regimes, one of which does superpose — which sharpened a research question rather than supporting it.

Three misattributions were caught and logged, all from the same cause: trusting our own earlier summary as if it were the source. The gap claim has been narrowed twice under evidence, and now reads as what survives scrutiny rather than what would sound strongest. The citation audit closed 27 August — this track is not looking for more papers.

The one thing that still gates everything

A sign error meant the controller added the interaction force it was supposed to cancel — reinforcing every disturbance instead of removing it, measured at exactly 2.00× on a known input in simulation. The correction was written, and then it flew: every flight carrying it diverged.

That reversal is the honest state of this project right now. The correction is believed right on paper and behaved badly in the air, and both can be true at once — it does not only change thrust, it moves the commanded attitude. So it has been parked at the value that flew for weeks, kept behind a runtime switch, and will be re-introduced one flight at a time rather than assumed.

Update, 11 September: the staged re-validation happened. Stage 1 (the restored configuration, unchanged) flew clean on both controllers — so the restoration itself is confirmed good, and attribution among the three original root causes no longer matters, since all three are reverted and the result is clean either way. What's still open is different: a residual, trajectory-tracking-only attitude oscillation on full INDI that survived four separate gain-tuning attempts (88°→7° peak, never a clean pass). The two-drone residual-sign A/B (res_sign=-1) — the decisive flight every downstream number depends on — has still not happened on hardware; it's queued behind isolating this oscillation via the H0 partition (attitude-INDI-only vs position-INDI-only), planned next.

The comparison against the cited paper's own code (10 September) held up: the two INDI implementations are confirmed the same law written two ways, not a bug on our side. The filter bug it surfaced is fixed and flight-confirmed safe. The gain-magnitude lead it raised (our attitude loop's natural frequency inside the observed oscillation band) is the hypothesis the 09-11 bisection was actually testing — real progress, not yet a resolution.

Next — in the lab

Strictly ordered. Each rung exists so that a failure has exactly one explanation.

  1. Bench check

    Confirm the position gain on the drone is the flight-proven value, not the simulator's. Tape-measure the flight volume — scenarios sit within ~10 cm of the walls.

  2. Re-validate both controllers, stage 1

    Run 09-11: three geometric flights + one restored-config INDI hover, all clean. The restoration is confirmed good on hardware.

  3. Stage 2 — isolate the residual oscillation

    Four gain-bisection attempts (09-11) brought peak trajectory-tracking oscillation from 88° to 7° but never reached a clean pass; a notch-filter attempt crashed and is parked pending offline root-cause. Next: the H0 partition (attitude-INDI-only vs position-INDI-only) to find which sub-loop carries it — never actually run yet. This is where the project actually is.

  4. Prove the residual sign

    Two drones stacked, compensation on versus off. Enabling it must shrink the separation error. This is the decisive flight — still ahead.

  5. Trajectory ladder

    Figure-8 on the old upload path, then the new one, then a circle. The new path has 180+ hover flights and multiple trajectory flights now (09-11) — this buys the right to trust it.

  6. Two drones, wide first

    Widest separation, then tighter. First 2-drone flight planned for the next session (rigid vertical formation, geometric, uSD logging on both drones) — confirm the residual grows as the gap closes, that is what shows it measures the interaction rather than noise.

  7. Freeze the gains

    Written down and unchanged from here. Re-tuning later would measure tuning effort rather than the methods being compared. Not yet reached — stage 2's oscillation question comes first.

Then data collection — vertical and lateral scenarios. A vertical-only dataset has no lateral variation at all, and the model would be extrapolating the first time a formation moves sideways.

Next — on paper

The writing track is blocked on flight data, not idle by choice. Everything that could be written without measurements has been: Chapters 1–5 are drafted, revised and accuracy-audited, and frozen. Chapters 6–9 are the results, discussion and conclusion — they need the comparison campaign to exist. Chapter 5 turns to past tense once it has been run. One paper remains abstract-only behind a paywall — the closest published work to this setting, and the single verification gap that cannot be closed by reading harder.

Three of the seven strategies are deliberately not implemented: one waits on code from its authors, two need a model that only real flight data can train, and two reinforcement-learning variants are explicitly a stretch goal rather than part of the contribution.
