What the literature work actually changed

Reading the papers in full rather than from abstracts moved several claims. A headline 36 % improvement turned out to be lateral, not three-dimensional — the paper contradicts its own abstract. A 5 ms control loop cited as evidence of onboard feasibility runs off-board on a laptop. A result read as refuting force superposition in fact reports three regimes, one of which does superpose — which sharpened a research question rather than supporting it.

Three misattributions were caught and logged, all from the same cause: trusting our own earlier summary as if it were the source. The gap claim has been narrowed twice under evidence, and now reads as what survives scrutiny rather than what would sound strongest. The citation audit closed 27 August — this track is not looking for more papers.

The one thing that still gates everything

A sign error meant the controller added the interaction force it was supposed to cancel — reinforcing every disturbance instead of removing it. It measured at exactly 2.00× on a known input. It is fixed in code and has still never flown.

It cannot be seen with one drone: a_res is essentially zero when there is nothing to interact with. The first real lab session (2026-09-07) flew single-drone hover on both controllers and surfaced a different, real bug first — a growing attitude oscillation, now root-caused and reverted. That means the sign-check flight itself is still ahead, not behind: two drones stacked, compensation on versus off, and enabling it must make separation error shrink. Every downstream number depends on that one flight coming out right.

Next — in the lab

Strictly ordered. Each rung exists so that a failure has exactly one explanation.

  1. Bench check

    Confirm the position gain on the drone is the flight-proven value, not the simulator's. Tape-measure the flight volume — scenarios sit within ~10 cm of the walls.

  2. Single-drone hover

    Flown 2026-09-07. Found a real regression (growing attitude oscillation) — root-caused to one config flag and reverted. Re-confirmation of clean hover is the very next thing to check, before trusting anything past this rung.

  3. Prove the residual sign

    Two drones stacked, compensation on versus off. Enabling it must shrink the separation error. This is the decisive flight — still ahead.

  4. Trajectory ladder

    Figure-8 on the old upload path, then the new one, then a circle. The new path has 180 hover flights but only one trajectory flight — this buys the right to trust it.

  5. Two drones, wide first

    Widest separation, then tighter. Confirm the residual grows as the gap closes — that is what shows it measures the interaction rather than noise.

  6. Freeze the gains

    Written down and unchanged from here. Re-tuning later would measure tuning effort rather than the methods being compared.

Then data collection — vertical and lateral scenarios. A vertical-only dataset has no lateral variation at all, and the model would be extrapolating the first time a formation moves sideways.

Next — on paper

Nothing blocks the writing track, and nothing is left in it that does not need data. Chapters 6–9 need the comparison campaign. Chapter 5 turns to past tense once it has been run. One paper remains abstract-only behind a paywall — the closest published work to this setting, and the single verification gap that cannot be closed by reading harder.

Three of the seven strategies are deliberately not implemented: one waits on code from its authors, two need a model that only real flight data can train, and two reinforcement-learning variants are explicitly a stretch goal rather than part of the contribution.
