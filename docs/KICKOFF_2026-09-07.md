# Master Thesis Kick-off — 7 Sep 2026

**Comparison of Control Strategies for Interaction-Force Aware Multirotor Teams**
Georg Wolnik, supervised by Prof. Wolfgang Hönig. Timeline: 1 Sep 2026 → end of Feb 2027 (~6 months).

---

## 1. One-paragraph problem

Multirotors flying close together generate downwash and interaction forces that standard
controllers do not model. The field has three families of response: reactive (measure the
disturbance and cancel it), predictive (learn the disturbance from relative state and feed it
forward), and hybrid combinations of the two. Each has been validated on its own platform, its
own metrics, its own team size. There is no controlled comparison of these families on the same
airframe, the same trajectories, the same formations, and frozen gains.

Four research questions:
- **RQ1** How do the strategies compare on identical hardware, trajectories, and gains?
- **RQ2** In which interaction regime (separation, speed, team size) does each strategy's
  advantage appear or disappear?
- **RQ3** What is the cost of going from 2 robots to 3 — not whether pairwise superposition
  holds, but how much each strategy loses when it doesn't?
- **RQ4** What does each strategy cost (compute, data, tuning effort) for what it buys?

What this thesis does **not** claim: a proof that any one family is universally better, a
result on hardware beyond this Crazyflie platform, or a claim about strategies never flown here.

Strategy 5's form: $a_{des} = \ldots - \hat{a}_{res} - (a_{res} - \hat{a}_{res})$ — the network
predicts the bulk, INDI corrects the remainder. Independently subtracting both terms instead of
this combined form is rejected as a design.

The core campaign is Strategies 1/2/3/5 — identical substrate, same attitude allocator. Strategy 4
follows the topic sheet but uses a structurally different control law, not a term added to
$a_{des}$, and only enters once author code arrives.

## 5. Papers

### 5.1 Primary / core (must sit in Related Work)

- `lee2010geometrictracking` / `lee2010geometricse3` — Lee et al., geometric $SE(3)$ tracking (Strategy 1 family)
- `tal2018indi` — Tal & Karaman, INDI (Strategy 2 family; encoders on their vehicle)
- `smeur2017cascadedindi` — Smeur et al., cascaded position/attitude INDI (single vehicle, wind/GPS)
- `shi2020neuralswarm` — Shi et al., Neural-Swarm (predictive family)
- `shi2022neuralswarm2` — Shi et al., Neural-Swarm2 (predictive family, heterogeneous teams)
- `cobobriesewitz2026lindi` — Cobo-Briesewitz et al., NA-INDI / LINDI (Strategy 5 family, single vehicle)
- `hsieh2026flatness` — Hsieh et al., flatness-preserving FBL (Strategy 4 family; hardware demo, off-board 5 ms loop)
- `yang2025flatnessresiduals` — Yang et al., flatness-preserving residual theory (Strategy 4's theory; simulation-only, planar quadrotor)

### 5.2 Secondary (helps; not the comparison set)

- `smith2023so2equivariant` — SO(2)-equivariant downwash
- `gielis2023aggregate` — aggregate / load-stand
- `shankar2023docking` — docking
- `gu2025comparative` — online estimators, sim
- `abro2025fractional` — fractional-order LQR, not our family
- `kiran2025downwash` / `bauersfeld2024airflow` — aerodynamic characterisation

Numbers to keep, with their qualifiers intact: Neural-Swarm's four-vehicle-trained network
reaches \SI{2.4}{cm} height error (two-vehicle-trained: 2.7 cm) against a 9.4 cm baseline;
Neural-Swarm2 flies sixteen robots at a minimum vertical distance of 24 cm. Smith reports 36%
average reduction in 3D tracking error and 56% in vertical tracking error — the paper is
internally inconsistent on whether the 36% figure is 3D or lateral; follow the abstract, not the
contributions list. Hsieh (Strategy 4)'s abstract reports a 31% reduction in average tracking
error; the body does not restate that aggregate, reporting instead 24% RMSE and a further 29%
RMSE across two hardware comparisons, at an off-board 5 ms loop rate. Cobo-Briesewitz report ~40%
reduction **without a payload**; with one, the picture changes and is not summarised by that
single number.

Citation audit tagged `citation-audit-2026-08-27`; the `.tex` is the source of truth, not `docs/02`.
