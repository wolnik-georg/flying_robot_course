# Neural-Swarm2 — Shi et al., 2022

## 0. Identity

- Full title: Neural-Swarm2: Planning and Control of Heterogeneous Multirotor Swarms Using Learned Interactions
- Authors: Guanya Shi, Wolfgang Hönig, Xichen Shi, Yisong Yue, Soon-Jo Chung
- Year / venue / DOI: IEEE Transactions on Robotics, Vol. 38, No. 2, April 2022. DOI 10.1109/TRO.2021.3098436. No arXiv id printed on this file.
- Version of THIS file: journal T-RO paper (IEEE Xplore PDF), not a conference preprint.
- Suggested cite key: `shi2022neuralswarm2`
- What this paper IS: residual $f_{a,z}$ learned with heterogeneous deep sets, then used in offline multi-robot planning and in an onboard geometric tracking law with motor-delay compensation.
- What this paper is NOT: not Neural-Swarm (ICRA 2020, 5 homogeneous robots). Not INDI. Not FBL. Not residual RL. Not online force estimation. Not a docking paper.

## 1. Problem they pose

Their wording: close-proximity heterogeneous swarms create "complex aerodynamic interaction forces, such as downwash generated by nearby drones and ground effect." Conventional planning and control "neglect capturing these interaction forces, resulting in sparse swarm configuration." Prior Crazyflie work needed large safety distance (they cite 60 cm for Crazyflie 2.0).

Plain restatement: without a residual wake model you must fly far apart or you track poorly. They want a compact, type-aware residual that a small MCU can evaluate, then use it in both planning and control.

## 2. Paper map (follow their TOC)

### I. Introduction

- Purpose: motivate close-proximity heterogeneous flight and state contributions.
- Construction: residual DNN + nominal physics. Two tools: spectral normalization, heterogeneous deep sets. Residual used in planner and controller.
- Key equation: none numbered here.
- Assumption: downwash of two small Crazyflies hovering 30 cm apart is "only 9 g," so it is flyable if modelled.
- Result: Fig. 1 caption claims min vertical distance 24 cm on 16 heterogeneous robots. Abstract claims "up to three times reduction in worst-case tracking errors" vs a baseline nonlinear tracker.

### II. Related Work

- Purpose: place the paper vs ground-effect models, two-rotor / two-vehicle aero, CNN/GNN encodings, conservative collision shapes.
- Construction: they argue deep sets beat CNNs (no grid, less data) and GNNs (no comms). Heterogeneous deep set claimed more compact than Rivière et al. GLAS encoding.
- Key equation: none.
- Assumption: propeller-velocity-field models do not generalize to multi-robot or heterogeneous teams and are stationary-only.
- Result: they claim this is "the first model for aerodynamic interactions between two or more multirotors in flight." Also first use of such a model in both planning and control. Contribution list vs [11]: heterogeneous deep sets + proof, two-stage planner, motor-delay compensation, 16-robot / 24 cm result.

### III. Problem Statement

- Purpose: write single-vehicle and swarm dynamics, then the OCP they will not solve in real time.
- Construction: state $\mathbf{x}=[\mathbf{p};\mathbf{v};\mathbf{R};\boldsymbol{\omega}]$. Disturbance force $\mathbf{f}_a$ and torque $\boldsymbol{\tau}_a$. First-order motor lag. Neighbours grouped by type. Environment is a special type. Interaction volume $\mathcal{V}$ is a hand-designed cuboid.
- Key equations (paper numbering):

$$
\begin{align}
\dot{\mathbf{p}}&=\mathbf{v},\qquad
m\dot{\mathbf{v}}=m\mathbf{g}+\mathbf{R}\mathbf{f}_u+\mathbf{f}_a \tag{1a}\\
\dot{\mathbf{R}}&=\mathbf{R}S(\boldsymbol{\omega}),\qquad
\mathbf{J}\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}=\mathbf{J}\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{\omega}+\boldsymbol{\tau}_u+\boldsymbol{\tau}_a \tag{1b}\\
\boldsymbol{\eta}&=\mathbf{B}_0\mathbf{u},\qquad
\dot{\mathbf{u}}=-\lambda\mathbf{u}+\lambda\mathbf{u}_c \tag{1c}
\end{align}
$$

Compact swarm form (4). OCP (6): min integrated $\|\mathbf{u}\|$ with dynamics, input/state sets, spherical collision $\|\mathbf{p}^{(ij)}\|\ge r^{\mathcal{I}(i)\mathcal{I}(j)}$, and explicit bounds $\|\mathbf{f}_a^{(i)}\|\le f_{a,\max}^{(i)}$, $\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_a^{(i)}\|\le\tau_{a,\max}^{(i)}$. Success metric (7):

$$
\max_{i,t}\|\mathbf{p}^{(i)}(t)-\mathbf{p}_d^{(i)}(t)\|.
$$

- Assumption: same type shares $\Phi$, $\mathbf{f}_a$, $\boldsymbol{\tau}_a$. Tight sphere collision instead of conservative ellipsoids/cylinders.
- Result: they will not solve (6) online. They split into offline planning + online tracking.

### IV. Learning of Swarm Aerodynamic Interaction

- Purpose: define the residual network and how it is trained.
- Construction: homogeneous deep set first (8), then $K$-group permutation invariance (Def. 1) and Theorem 4. Implemented as (9). Spectral normalization of every $\phi$ and $\rho$ layer. Curriculum data collection (2 vehicles, then more, with current residual already in the loop).
- Key equation (9):

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{f}_a^{\mathcal{I}(i)}\\\boldsymbol{\tau}_a^{\mathcal{I}(i)}\end{bmatrix}
\approx
\rho_{\mathcal{I}(i)}\Biggl(
\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\sum_{\mathbf{x}^{(ij)}\in\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{type}_k}^{(i)}}
\phi_{\mathcal{I}(j)}(\mathbf{x}^{(ij)})
\Biggr).
$$

Lipschitz bound via $W_i\leftarrow W_i/\|W_i\|_2\cdot\gamma^{1/(L+1)}$ (11).

- Assumption: residual is a continuous $K$-group permutation-invariant map of relative states. Later they drop $\boldsymbol{\tau}_a$ and $f_{a,x},f_{a,y}$ (experiment section).
- Result: architecture can take any neighbour count. Body: trained on 1–3 vehicles, "good predictions for five." Theorem 4 is representation, not a flight result.

### V. Interaction-Aware Multirobot Planning

- Purpose: approximate (6) offline.
- Construction: plan one robot at a time. Double-integrator plus learned $\hat{\mathbf{f}}_a$ in the state. Stage 1: modified AO-RRT with force bounds and a trust region on neighbours' $\hat{\mathbf{f}}_a$. Stage 2: SCP with linearized residual Jacobians (autograd). Algorithm 1.
- Key equations: simplified dynamics (12); Euler (13); linearizations (14)–(15); convex program (16).
- Assumption: sequential planning is complete only for well-formed infrastructure. Other robots treated as known moving obstacles. Constant $\Delta t$.
- Result: Fig. 6–7. Planning without NN picks stacked geometries that violate force limits (example: small-vehicle disturbance "5 g" exceeded; SL case "greater than 10 g"). Planning with NN keeps forces inside the chosen bounds and cuts worst-case tracking error in the planning study (body: ignoring interaction "can result in errors of over 10 cm").

### VI. Interaction-Aware Tracking Controller

- Purpose: track any $\mathbf{p}_d(t)$, planned or not.
- Construction: hierarchical geometric position + attitude (Bandyopadhyay / Shi lineage) with $-\hat{\mathbf{f}}_a$ and $-\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_a$ in the wrench, plus delay inversion (21).
- Key equations:

$$
\mathbf{v}_r=\dot{\mathbf{p}}_d-\Lambda_p\tilde{\mathbf{p}} \tag{17}
$$

$$
\mathbf{f}_d=-m\mathbf{g}+m\dot{\mathbf{v}}_r-\hat{\mathbf{f}}_a-(K_v+m\Gamma_v)\tilde{\mathbf{v}}-K_v\Gamma_v\int\tilde{\mathbf{v}} \tag{18}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\tau}_d=\mathbf{J}\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_r-\mathbf{J}\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{\omega}_r-\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_a-K_\omega\tilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-\Gamma_\omega\int\tilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}} \tag{20}
$$

$$
\mathbf{u}_c=\mathbf{B}_0^+\Bigl(\boldsymbol{\eta}_d+\dot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_d/\lambda\Bigr) \tag{21}
$$

Position error ball (22):

$$
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\tilde{\mathbf{p}}\|
=\frac{\bar{d}_f}{\lambda_{\min}(\Lambda_p)\,\lambda_{\min}(\Gamma_v)\,\lambda_{\min}(K_v)}.
$$

Theorems 5–7 under Assumption 1 (uniform bounds on residual error and its rate).

- Assumption: bounded $\epsilon_f,\epsilon_\tau,\dot{\epsilon}_f,\dot{\epsilon}_\tau$. Fully or over-actuated. $\lambda$ known from bench tests. In hardware they approximate $\dot{\tau}_d$ by an extra $-K_{\dot{\omega}}\dot{\tilde{\omega}}$ term; other $\dot{\tau}_d$ pieces "negligible."
- Result: stability statements only. Flight numbers live in Sec. VII.

### VII. Experiments

- Purpose: identify plants, collect labels, ablate nets, fly planning cases and hardware swaps/rings.
- Construction: load-cell thrust maps $\psi_1,\psi_2,\psi_3$; $\lambda=16$ both sizes. 12 scenarios (Table II). Loss (24) on stacked robots/scenarios. Only $f_{a,z}$ learned. Inputs: relative $\mathbf{p},\mathbf{v}$ in world frame ($\mathbb{R}^6$). Env neighbour: $\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}$.
- Key numbers live in Tables I–III and Figs. 5–9. See §§4–5 below.
- Assumption: 5-point numerical derivative of state gives labels after subtracting identified $\Phi$. Delay included when forming labels.
- Result (hardware, body): L2S median worst-case $z$-error 17 cm $\to$ 7 cm. Five-robot rings: max $z$-error 10 cm $\to$ 5 cm. Sixteen-robot rings: max $z$-error of a small vehicle 15 cm $\to$ 10 cm; min vertical distance 24 cm. Online estimate-and-compensate "would quickly crash the drone" (their observation, no plot).

### VIII. Conclusion

- Purpose: restate claims. No new experiment.
- Result restated: 2–16 robots, two sizes; "reduces the worst-case height error by a factor of two or better." That phrasing is conclusion-level, not a new measurement.

## 6. Assumptions and failure modes

- Residual map is continuous and $K$-group permutation-invariant.
- Interaction volume is a user cuboid, not learned.
- Approximation error and its time derivative are uniformly bounded (Assumption 1). Bound is not measured online.
- Only $f_{a,z}$ is used in the trained law. $\boldsymbol{\tau}_a$ "very small" vs $K_\omega\tilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}$. Lateral residuals "much smaller and less varied."
- Curriculum flights are required; free-space data are not enough because the uncompensated team flies apart.
- Sequential planner + known neighbour trajectories. Not real-time distributed replanning.
- Mocap. Relative onboard sensing not tested.
- $\lambda$ from bench test. Small $\lambda$ error still "robust" per their text, not quantified.
- They state online disturbance estimation "would quickly crash the drone."
- At 16 vehicles the residual is "relatively less accurate." Error still drops, but this is the generalization stress case.
- Some swap trials used upgraded small motors (S*). That is not the stock 34 g / 65 g row.

## 11. 12-line abstract I can reread

Neural-Swarm2 learns a residual vertical force $f_{a,z}$ for heterogeneous multirotors with a deep-set encoder per neighbour type (small, large, ground) and a decoder per ego type. The net is spectrally normalized. Inputs are relative position and velocity. Torque and lateral force are not learned. The residual is used in an offline two-stage planner (AO-RRT then SCP) and in an onboard geometric tracker that also inverts a first-order motor lag. Training used 1–3 vehicles and 1.4 million labelled pairs. Hardware is Crazyflie-class small (34 g) and a larger 67 g frame. Baseline is the same tracker with residual set to zero. Abstract: up to 3× lower worst-case tracking error, and separately a 16-robot flight at 24 cm min vertical. Body for that 16-robot flight: max $z$-error 15 cm → 10 cm. Other body numbers: L2S 17→7 cm, five-robot rings 10→5 cm. Residual evaluation ran on the STM32. The planner did not.

Source: attached PDF only. No other paper used.
