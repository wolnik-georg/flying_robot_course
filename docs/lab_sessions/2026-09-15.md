Built `find_flight_window.py` (cross-correlates measured position against the scenario's own
recomputed commanded trajectory to locate the real flight inside a long recording) and
`copy_usd_log.py` (safe, sha256-verified, wall-clock-named card extraction). Also caught and
fixed a bad assumption mid-session: a uSD log and a radio CSV that arrived together via
`git pull` are NOT necessarily the same flight (see `MEMORY.md`,
`feedback_usd_radio_log_matching_2026-09-15.md`) — they were a night apart.

**Verification flight → repeated hard crashes.** With all of the above fixed, ran a fresh A8 to
verify the chain end-to-end. Instead: both drones went "full nuts" after the swap/crossing,
every single attempt, regardless of `passes` (tried 1, 2, and 3). Two drones, four separate
flights, same failure signature every time: clean ramp-up, then a violent tumble (roll to
~180° on `cf_second`, large `vel_z`) at almost exactly the same *elapsed flight time* (t≈11–13s)
no matter how long the scenario itself was supposed to run.

**First theory (real bug, but NOT the cause): a controller-identity race in `apply()`.**
`formation_flight.py`,
`run_formation.py`, and `flight.py` all share the same phase-transition helper: it broadcasts
the shared `stabilizer.controller`/`indi_gains`/`pos_gains` to every drone in one loop, then
re-pushes each drone's own `per_robot` override (e.g. `cf_second`'s permanent stock-Lee pin,
`cf231_active`'s temporary geometric pin) in a second, separate loop. Those are two distinct
radio round-trips, not one atomic write — in the gap between them, a pinned drone genuinely ran
the SHARED controller (tonight: our OOT/INDI controller, `ctrl_mode=3`) with gains tuned for a
different airframe's mass/kt, for however long the round-trip took. That is a real, physical
bad-torque command, not a logging artifact: `cf_second`'s own `indi.a_res_z` spiked to ~‑8/‑9
m/s² at exactly the tumble onset, confirming a genuine unmodelled force, not corrupted
telemetry. Because `apply('scenario', ...)` always fires at the same point in the sequence
(right after trajectory upload, before `startTrajectory`), the glitch lands at the same elapsed
time regardless of scenario length — explaining why 1/2/3-pass flights all broke identically.
The battery-voltage sag that first looked like a possible cause was correctly identified as a
symptom, not the cause (the violent attitude correction is what draws the current that sags
the battery, not the other way round).

**Fixed in all three files** (`crazyswarm2` `9d9c3de`). `apply()`/`_apply_config()` now skip a
param in the shared broadcast loop entirely whenever that drone has its own `per_robot`
override for it. This is a genuine bug and the fix is correct and kept — but the re-flight
after it crashed identically, so it was **not** what was causing the tumbles.

**ACTUAL ROOT CAUSE: `usec.reset` broadcast mid-flight destroyed the HLC time base.**
(`crazyswarm2` `e07342f`.) The decisive test was flying **A1** — a pure two-drone hover with no
trajectory motion whatsoever — through the same script. It failed the same way (onset t=8.8s,
roll to 176°, |vel_z| 10.5 m/s), which ruled out trajectory content, the swap geometry,
downwash, `passes`, `dz`, controller choice and gains all at once, and pointed squarely at the
fixed setup sequence every flight runs.

The mechanism: the high-level commander's entire time base is `usecTimestamp()`
(`crtp_commander_high_level.c:352`, `float t = usecTimestamp() / 1e6;`), and the planner stores
`t_begin` from that same clock when takeoff/goTo starts (`planner.c`,
`p->planned_trajectory.t_begin = t`). The `usec.reset` broadcast — added 2026-09-14 to
`formation_flight.py`/`flight.py`, and added to `run_formation.py` earlier the same evening —
sat **mid-flight**, immediately before `usd.logging=1`. Zeroing the timer there leaves
`t_begin` holding a large value, so `piecewise_eval` computes `t - t_begin` ≈ *minus* several
hundred seconds, evaluates a degree-7 polynomial far outside its domain, and hands the
controller an astronomically wrong setpoint. Motors cut or the vehicle slams over within one
HLC tick.

This accounts for every observation that the earlier theories did not: the failure landed at a
**fixed elapsed time** (7.84–7.85 s on `cf231_active` across `passes`=1/2/3, within 30 ms)
regardless of scenario length, because the broadcast fires at a fixed point in the setup
sequence — not at a trajectory-dependent moment. It was independent of controller, gains and
environment. And it reproduced on a scenario with no motion at all.

Fix: broadcast `usec.reset` **once, on the ground, before takeoff**, in all three scripts.
There the planner is IDLE (no `t_begin` in use) and takeoff samples the clock *after* the
reset, so the time base stays monotonic for the whole flight. uSD logs keep their shared
origin — what mattered was that every drone's clock is zeroed together, not that logging starts
at exactly `t=0`. Consequence for analysis: `find_flight_window.py` should now expect a lag of
**~9–12 s** (takeoff + climb + converge + upload), not ~0; the tool and
`README_usd_thesis_logging.md` were updated accordingly. Measured on the validation flight:
**8.85 s** (bottom) and **8.88 s** (top).

**Verification flight, then the full chain — both done, see §2.** The post-fix A8 returned to
the pre-bug baseline exactly (max roll 34.08° vs 33.70°/33.96° on the two clean pre-bug
flights, full flight completed, no free-fall), and the uSD chain was then exercised end to end
on it.

## 2. uSD chain validated end-to-end, and the gaps closed after it

**First fully validated uSD flight (A8, 19:56), both drones.** `cf231_active` (bottom,
`thesis06`) and `cf_second` (top, `thesis08`): 500 Hz with a 2.3 ms worst-case gap, both
matching their commanded trajectory unambiguously at 1.9 / 1.8 cm RMS, cross-drone clock
agreement ~20–30 ms, and `indi.a_res_*` non-zero throughout. Realised geometry from the merged
500 Hz data: `dz` 0.193–0.283 m against a commanded 0.25, closest approach 0.198 m at t=4.96 s,
bottom vehicle's `a_res_z` reaching −3.36 m/s² there (−4.98 peak). That is the downwash
interaction the thesis measures, recorded correctly on both vehicles for the first time.

Gaps found while validating, and how each was closed:

1. **Alignment.** `merge_usd_logs.py`'s z-cross-correlation is unreliable whenever a scenario
   holds both vehicles at near-constant altitude — A8 exactly — reporting +262 ms at corr 0.39
   where the truth was ~20 ms. Added `--meta`/`--roles`, which aligns each drone against *its
   own* commanded trajectory. That is now authoritative; z-correlation is a flagged cross-check.
2. **Identity.** Drone names in filenames are operator-supplied, and cards get moved between
   vehicles, so the label was never evidence. The merge now aborts if a log's fit to its
   claimed role exceeds 15 cm RMS — verified by deliberately swapping roles (79.5 cm, refused).
3. **Which run.** `find_flight_window.py` proves a file holds *a run* of a scenario, not *which*
   run: two identical back-to-back A8s matched both meta files at 1.7/1.8 cm. The tool now says
   so explicitly. Ordering repeated runs relies on the `thesisNN` counter — an assumption about
   session order, not a measurement.
4. **0-byte files, root-caused** from `usddeck.c`'s `usdWriteTask`: a **new file is created on
   every `usd.logging` 0→1**, and `f_close` runs only on 1→0. So a 0-byte `thesisNN` is a
   session that started but never stopped cleanly (crash, power loss, card pulled) — not a
   fault. This also corrects an earlier claim in `README_usd_thesis_logging.md` that sessions
   append into one file per power cycle; they do not. It further explains why the *old*
   `check_usd_deck.py` appeared to "fix" missing logs: its 1→0 toggle forced a clean close.
5. **Two tool bugs in `find_flight_window.py`**, both found against real data: the lag search
   ignored recordings whose clock does not start near zero, and the minimum-overlap guard was
   100 samples (0.2 s at 500 Hz) — together they produced a confident *"STRONG match, 173×
   better than runner-up"* fitted to 0.2 s of tail. Now requires ≥80% scenario coverage and
   reports RMS in cm. Also fixed `merge_usd_logs.py`'s name parsing, which truncated every real
   drone name (`cf231_active`→`cf231`, `cf_second`→`cf`).

## 3. Per-drone controller assignment is intended — only the logging was wrong

Clarified at the end of the session. The design is deliberate and stays:

- **`cf_second` is permanently pinned to stock Lee (`controller: 5`)** — it exists as the other
  formation member and the downwash source, not as a controller under study.
- **`cf231_active` is the vehicle under study** and carries its own `ctrl_mode`/`pos_gains`.
- Drones may run the same controller or different ones; `robots.<name>.firmware_params` is the
  supported, first-class way to say so, and it already wins over the shared `all:` broadcast on
  the wire (that precedence is what `apply()` enforces, fixed earlier tonight).

**What was actually broken was the recording, not the flying.** The shared `all:` block was
written verbatim into *every* drone's log, so a pinned drone's log claimed a config it never
ran — every A8 since 09-14 recorded `ctrl_mode=3` / `pos_gains` 64/5/48/7 for `cf231_active`,
which actually flew `ctrl_mode=0` / 40/8/30/10. Proof from the uSD data: `tau_x/y/z` are exactly
0.0 across all 7637 samples, i.e. the INDI torque path never executed.

Fixed in `run_formation.py` and `formation_flight.py` (`crazyswarm2` `a7d5d0f`): each drone's
log now carries its own **resolved** config, plus `config_source` (`all` vs
`all+robot_override`), `shared_controller`/`shared_ctrl_mode` for reference, and `gains_apply=0`
when the drone runs a stock firmware controller that ignores `indi_gains`/`pos_gains` entirely.
`run_formation.py`'s sidecar gains a `per_drone` section, and both scripts print the per-drone
table at startup so a mismatch is visible *before* takeoff. No flight behaviour changed.

**On logging the upper drone at all:** worth keeping regardless of its controller. `cf_second`
has no `a_res` of its own (stock Lee does not compute it), but its position/velocity are exactly
what the relative-state input needs — the bottom drone's residual is not interpretable without
knowing where the top drone was.

## 7. Open questions

- Does the same controller-identity race exist anywhere else that broadcasts params outside
  `apply()`/`_apply_config()` (e.g. any one-off `setParam` calls elsewhere in these three
  files)? Not audited beyond the three `apply` functions themselves.
- **`cf231_active`'s radio log came out entirely zero on 2 of 10 flights** (18-53-15,
  19-19-47) — right row count (301, 487), every value zero. **Not** correlated with crashes
  (19-19-47 flew fine; the 18-54-27 crash logged normally), only ever this one drone, and not
  reproducible on demand.
  **Mechanism now identified** (`DroneLogger._state`): rows are appended from the `/state`
  callback, and every field is read from `self.latest`, a `defaultdict(float)`. If `msg.values`
  arrives **empty or short**, the `zip()` updates nothing and the row is written entirely from
  defaults — a full-length, plausible-looking, all-zero log. The messages arrived at the normal
  rate, so the subscription was fine; the payload was not, i.e. **the log block did not start
  correctly on that drone**.
  **Still unknown:** *why* the block fails, on that one drone, intermittently. Now instrumented
  rather than guessed at — the logger warns on the **first** short message, naming the drone and
  the actual payload length, which is the evidence needed to finish this if it recurs. The
  save-time all-zero warning stays as a backstop. The uSD log is unaffected in this mode.
- ~~`cf231_active`'s stale `ctrl_mode: 0` override…~~ **RESOLVED** — see §3c. The override is
  *kept deliberately* (per-drone controller assignment is the intended design); what was wrong
  was only the logging, now fixed.
- **Two distinct attitude events, initially conflated (corrected after looking at the 500 Hz uSD
  data).** They are not the same thing and only one is a defect:
  1. **Handover transient**, radio t≈7.87 s: roll swings +15.2°/−19.5°, `gyro_x` to −474 °/s,
     altitude dips ~4 cm, damps out within ~0.5 s. Occurs at `apply('scenario', ...)`, i.e. the
     controller/gain switch, and therefore *before* `usd.logging` starts — it is visible only in
     the radio logs. Pre-existing (present in the pre-bug baselines) and unrelated to the
     `usec.reset` bug. This is the real open item worth fixing.
  2. **Crossing disturbance — this is the thesis signal, not a fault.** The ~34° peak is not at
     the handover at all. At both crossings the bottom vehicle takes a large roll excursion
     ≈0.2 s *after* closest approach, with opposite sign each time (the passes are in opposite
     directions), coincident with a strongly negative `a_res_z`:

     | | closest approach | peak roll |
     |---|---|---|
     | crossing 1 | t=4.96 s, \|d\|=0.198 m, `a_res_z`=−3.36 | t=5.14 s, +30.6° |
     | crossing 2 | t=10.95 s, \|d\|=0.213 m, `a_res_z`=−2.68 | t=11.15 s, −34.4° |

     That is the downwash interaction A8 exists to measure, showing up cleanly and symmetrically
     in both the residual and the attitude response.
