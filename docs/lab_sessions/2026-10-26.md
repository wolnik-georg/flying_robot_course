### What differs from the authors' FBL in the firmware (FBL only; S0–S3 are not affected)
Changed 2026-10-25: FBL now flies the **whole flight** (takeoff to landing, like S0–S3 fly their controller; before, the Omar controller flew below 0.15 m) and the extra safety limits are **off** (no 60° tilt latch, no 20 rad/s rate limit; the bundle has neither). Still in, because they are the real path, not additions: thrust limited to 0…max (the stock uint16 motor interface does the same) and a guard against non-numbers (never active in normal flight). The 10-24 behaviour can be restored with `ctrlFbl.z_on 0.15`, `z_off 0.10`, `tilt_max 60`, `w_max 20`.

**The one addition left: the downwash gate.** For each neighbour the authors' code computes the vertical distance z0 (without sign) and the horizontal distance r0, and adds the physics-model push (along +z, i.e. it assumes the neighbour is above) plus the network output, for every neighbour, always. Our firmware adds the term only if:
1. the neighbour is **above** by more than 0.05 m: the authors' code would push our drone down by the same −2.37 m/s² when the neighbour is **below** (z0 has no sign), which is physically wrong (the downwash goes down);
2. the horizontal distance is **below 0.6 m**: there the physics model is already 0 (0.000 m/s² at r0 0.6), but the trained network extrapolates and grows to −0.49 m/s² at r0 1.0 m (it was trained only on rows within 0.5 m); also our physics model is a lookup table that ends at 0.6 m;
3. the vertical distance is **below 1.4 m**, faded out from 1.2 m: beyond our training range.

Effect in our cells: the top drone is always above and dz ≤ 0.75 m, so 1 and 3 never act; 2 acts only when the drones are far apart (A8 ends, A4 drift), where the bundle would add a wrong push from the network. In the paper's own test (stacked, r0 ≈ 0) the gate never acts.
