## 2. The geometric-crash root cause, in detail

Initially attributed to `kr_geo`/`kw_geo` being brushless-specific and never validated for
standard. **Checked with git history and that turned out to be wrong**: `kr_geo=0.010`/
`kw_geo=0.00110` is bit-identical to the compile-time constant active on standard back on
2026-07-02 (commit `b8bd603`, "all flights stable") — that exact number did fly clean on
standard before. The real cause: everything *around* that gain in the shared
`all.firmware_params` block (`fc_bw=206`, `clamp_en=11`, `res_fc`/`res_clamp`, `tau_xy_max`,
`thrust_max`, notch settings) has been iterated on and validated only against brushless since
July, and standard inherited the whole current configuration the moment it was enabled — not
just one old, still-valid gain sitting in isolation. This is a genuine finding worth
remembering: shared-`all:` configs drift toward whichever platform is actively being tuned,
silently, and re-enabling a dormant platform inherits ALL of that drift at once, not none of
it.

## 6. Desk-time follow-up (same day, after the lab closed)

1. **Notch crash root-caused and fixed offline (NOT YET FLOWN).** The 2026-07-29 fix hoisted
   `alpha_meas`/`alpha_ref` notch-filter warm-up out of the mode-gated branch to kill a
   controller-handover cold-start transient, but missed a third chain: `tau_current`'s own
   Butterworth pre-filter (`bw_tau_x/y/z`) only started updating once `filt_tau`/`notch_en`
   went true — which lands on the same tick as the ctrl_mode handover in every flight script.
   Same transient class, same "always run, gate only the selection" fix, applied in
   `firmware_app/src/lib.rs` (`flying_robot_course` `bc7190c`). `DRONE=bl`/`DRONE=std`/host
   builds all verified clean and behavior-preserving at `notch_en=0`/`filt_tau=0` defaults.
   Needs a real `notch_en=1` flight before being trusted.
2. **The mixed-platform crash root cause turned out bigger than §2 recorded.** While
   generalizing today's controller/ctrl_mode per-robot fix, found that `apply()`'s broadcast
   also carries `indi_gains.mass`/`kt1-4` from the shared `all:` block — so `cf_second`'s
   per-robot mass/`kt` override (added earlier the same day specifically for the standard
   platform) was silently wiped by the very first `apply("takeoff", ...)` call, before takeoff
   even happened. `cf_second` almost certainly flew the crashed geometric flight on brushless's
   thrust model (mass 0.041 vs its real 0.0364 kg, `kt`≈4.1e-10 vs its real ≈1.4e-10, ~2.8× off)
   the whole time, not its own values as intended — this plausibly matters *more* than the
   shared-config-drift explanation in §2, though both are likely real contributors. Fixed
   generally: `load_per_robot_overrides()` now re-reads and re-pushes ANY per-robot
   `stabilizer`/`indi_gains`/`pos_gains` key after every broadcast (`crazyswarm2` `fdfc640`),
   so per-robot config always wins last for any future key, not just this one.
   **This bug explains `cf_second` (standard)'s own instability, not `cf231_active`
   (brushless)'s crash directly** — brushless's config was never touched by this bug (it's the
   platform everything is tuned against). Its crash 2s after leaving the ground, with a real
   `a_res_z` spike of 8.1 m/s² at the moment of divergence, reads as collateral damage from the
   drone thrashing directly above it, not a separate fault of its own — a working explanation
   from timing and the residual-force signature, not directly proven contact.
3. **Both web pages republished** (`Controller Validation Card`, `Two Tracks to the Thesis`)
   with today's full results, and `docs/07` updated with a new History entry (23) covering all
   of the above. Committed and pushed.
