RPM Source Quality

Does the optical RPM deck agree with DShot bidirectional telemetry on real C.1 flights? Bias, lag, and dropout across 38 merged logs (272 motor-rows) — not a stability claim, not a policy change.

Latency vs policy: the deck is faster — lag is reported as positive when DShot lags the deck (typically a few ms, often on a 2 ms grid). DShot feeds control for reliability (deck reported zero on 2/4 motors in real 2-drone flight), not because it is the lower-latency channel.

Metrics explained, step by step (real worked example)

Every number below is hand-computed from the raw merged CSV for one real motor-row — cf5, flight A3_2026-09-21_13-00-57, motor 2, the same flight/motor as the overlay plot below — to independently confirm the pipeline's own output. They match to the decimal.

The basic idea: every flight logs RPM from two separate sensors watching the same four motors — the optical deck (a camera-like sensor inferring speed from the spinning propeller) and DShot (the motor controller electronically reporting its own speed back over the wire that drives it). Neither is "ground truth" by construction — they're two independent measurements of the same physical thing, and disagreement between them is information, not automatically an error in either one. A motor-row is one flight × one drone × one motor — the unit everything below is computed on.

  1. Raw samples. At 500 Hz each motor-row is a long list of (deck, dshot) pairs. A normal stretch, both sensors near 19,000 RPM:
    t (s)deck (RPM)DShot (RPM)error
    5.00019044.019043.4−0.7
    5.00418985.818796.0−189.8
    5.01618734.418790.3+56.0
    Then, 100 ms later in the same motor-row, a spike:
    t (s)deck (RPM)DShot (RPM)error
    5.12016833.634893.5+18059.8
    5.12216803.549505.5+32702.0
    The deck barely moved. DShot nearly doubled, then nearly tripled, for two ticks, then came back. This pair of rows is what "spike" means throughout this doc.
  2. Two validity masks. deck > 0 excludes real dropout (deck reads exactly 0 when it loses lock — not noise, a missing reading). 0 < dshot < 60000 excludes DShot's own 0xFFFF "no value" sentinel with a safety margin. Samples passing both are base valid — the widest reasonable mask, used for bias_rpm/rmse_raw_rpm. Robust valid = base valid and |dshot−deck| ≤ 10,000 — drops exactly the two spike rows above. On this motor-row: 17,688 base-valid, 17,683 robust-valid — 5 samples excluded out of nearly 18,000.
  3. Bias — plain average of (dshot−deck) over every base-valid sample: bias_rpm = +2.2 here. DShot reads 2.2 RPM higher than the deck on average across the whole flight — on a ~15-19k RPM hover, that's rounding noise, not a real calibration mismatch. Averaging cancels random per-tick noise while preserving any genuine constant offset; there isn't one here.
  4. RMSE, and why two versions. Square every error, average the squares, take the square root. Squaring makes large errors count far more than small ones — exactly why raw and robust diverge so sharply: rmse_raw_rpm = 508.1 (two spike samples out of 17,688 dominate the sum of squares, making normal agreement look 3% off when it isn't) vs rmse_robust_rpm = 106.7 (same formula, spikes excluded — what the sensors actually agree to on a normal tick, about half a percent of hover RPM). The fleet headline (128.1 / 802.9 RPM median) is this same split repeated across all 272 motor-rows — this one worked example sits close to the fleet median on both counts, not a cherry-picked best case.
  5. Lag — cross-correlation: slide one trace against the other by every offset up to ±50 ms, find the offset where they line up best. That offset is the lag. Sign convention, verified not just asserted: a self-test builds a synthetic DShot trace by deliberately delaying the real deck trace by a known 10 ms, then confirms the function recovers exactly +10.00 ms — it does, every run. So when real flight data shows positive lag almost everywhere, that's a verified statement that DShot is the delayed channel, not an assumption.
  6. Dropout (deck_zero_pct/dshot_zero_pct) — simplest metric here: percentage of all samples where that sensor read exactly 0. This is what flagged the separate, real A2 top-drone deck dropout below — unrelated to the spike story above.

Honest correction (2026-09-30): these two panels were first captioned as showing "obvious alignment" after the shift — checked quantitatively (correlation/RMSE between deck and DShot in the exact plotted window) and that doesn't hold. A3's shift is marginally in the right direction (correlation 0.400→0.422) but too small to see by eye. A1's shift makes agreement measurably worse (correlation 0.716→0.435, RMSE 75→92 RPM) — its 44 ms flight-wide lag value is exactly the kind of noisy correlation-peak artifact the "Top vs bottom" section below already warns isn't a real calibrated delay. Several other 6-10 ms exemplars were checked as replacements and showed similarly marginal, inconsistent results — the likely real finding is that measurable lag in this dataset is mostly too small relative to sample noise to visibly demonstrate in a short window, not that a better example exists. Kept both plots for the record; read them as "the self-test proves the method is correct," not "these two panels show it working well."

Top vs bottom — a consistent pattern

Wherever both vehicles are logged, cf_second (top) shows larger and noisier cross-correlation lag than cf5 (bottom) — e.g. A1 top mean 4.7 ms vs bottom 0.7 ms; A7 top max 36 ms vs bottom 2 ms. Isolated extrema (44 ms on one A1-top motor-row) should be read as noisy correlation peaks, not calibrated actuator delay — most values still sit on the 2 ms sampling grid. Bias stays sub-percent everywhere except where deck dropout dominates (A2 top, above).

Thesis methods — draft paragraph

During C.1 data collection, onboard uSD logging recorded both optical-deck RPM (rpm.m1–4) and DShot bidirectional ESC RPM (motor.m1_rpm–m4_rpm) at 500 Hz on every flight, independent of the control path. Since 2026-09-16, the bottom (geometric) drone uses indi_gains.rpm_source=1 so DShot feeds torque reconstruction and logged indi.a_res_* — chosen for motor dropout reliability, not latency (DShot typically lags the deck by a few ms). Agreement uses spike-filtered RMSE 128 RPM median motor-row vs 803 RPM raw diagnostic; spikes are DShot-only (best-fit decode glitches, not confirmed). The deck stayed mounted for parallel logging. A2 top shows deck dropout; firmware rpm_get_all() guard protects future flights only.
