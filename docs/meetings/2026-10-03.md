## 3. RPM dual source: optical deck vs DShot
- Result: nearly identical; **DShot lags the deck by ≈2 ms**. DShot is used for control because it is reliable (the deck showed zero on 2 of 4 motors in a real flight), not because it is faster.
- **Spikes:** DShot sometimes jumps to 28 000–59 000 RPM (true value 12 000–17 000), a few ticks long, in every flight → **removed before the metrics**, and **blocked in the firmware**.
- Removal: a sample is dropped from both sources if the difference Δ = DShot − deck leaves its local range (|Δ − median of 21 samples| > max(600 RPM, 6·1.4826·MAD)), or |Δ| > 10 000 RPM, or DShot ≥ 60 000.
- Metrics (deck `d`, spike-free DShot `s`, same motor and flight, 500 Hz, airborne samples):
  - bias % = 100 · mean(`s − d`) / mean(`d`)
  - RMSE = √ mean( (`s − d`)² )
  - r = Pearson correlation of `d` and `s`
  - lag τ = shift in −50…+50 ms maximising the cross-correlation of the mean-removed traces; τ > 0 = DShot later
- All clean flights (101 motor-rows): bias −0.24 %, RMSE 94 RPM, r 0.98, lag 2 ms (medians).
