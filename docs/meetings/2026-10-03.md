## 3. RPM dual source: optical deck vs DShot
- Result: traces nearly identical; DShot ≈2 ms later (= 1 sample at 500 Hz). DShot is used for control because it is reliable, not faster.
- Data: top drone only (`cf_second`, geometric, no downwash), 8 flights × 4 motors = 32 motor-rows, airborne part only.
- Plot flight = the calmest one (RPM stays within ≈800 RPM, so no "unstable hover").

**Spike filter** (Δ = DShot − deck, per sample, 500 Hz)
- A DShot sample is a spike if **|Δ − median(Δ over ±10 samples)| > max(600 RPM, 6 · 1.4826 · MAD)** (MAD = median absolute deviation, so the limit ≈ 6σ of the normal noise ≈ 600 RPM), or **|Δ| > 10 000 RPM**, or **DShot ≥ 60 000 RPM**.
- Spikes seen: DShot jumps to 28 000–59 000 RPM while the true value is 12 000–17 000.
- Spike samples are dropped from **both** traces before any metric. Here: 254 of 434 000 samples (0.06 %), max 14 per motor per flight.
- Firmware has its own sanity filter, so spikes no longer reach control.

**Metrics** (deck `d`, filtered DShot `s`, N samples)
- bias % = 100 · mean(`s − d`) / mean(`d`)
- RMSE = √( mean( (`s − d`)² ) )
- r = Pearson correlation of `d` and `s` (`numpy.corrcoef`)
- lag τ: subtract the mean from `d` and `s`, compute `c[k] = Σ d[n+k] · s[n]` (`numpy.correlate`, full), take the shift k with the largest c within ±25 samples (±50 ms), τ = −k/500 Hz. τ > 0 = DShot later. Removed samples are linearly interpolated first. Resolution = 2 ms.

**Numbers** (median over 32 motor-rows): bias −0.22 %, RMSE 104 RPM, r 0.92, lag 2 ms.
- r is below 1 in the calm A8 flights because the real RPM barely changes there, so sensor noise (~100 RPM) dominates.
