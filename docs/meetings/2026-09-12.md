## 1. Summary

*Comparison of Control Strategies for Interaction-Force Aware Multirotor Teams.* Software
preparation is finished: geometric SE(3) and INDI controllers are implemented, tuned, and
commissioned on Crazyflie 2.1 hardware (standard, thrust-upgraded, and brushless); the
16-scenario formation library, the simulator, and the residual-learning pipeline are all built
and validated in simulation. Alongside the practical work, I've read the core papers multiple
times plus several supporting ones, and written summaries of each for re-reading. The project
now blocks on one thing: real multi-drone flight data. The lab has just cleared a major
milestone — the first-ever 2-drone flight — but a controller-validation question (a residual
oscillation under INDI trajectory tracking) is still open and the gains are not yet frozen,
which is what stands between here and starting the actual data-collection campaign (C.1).

**Controller & platform commissioning** (this spans many weeks, see `docs/07` History for the
full log):
- Geometric SE(3) and all 4 INDI modes (geometric / position-INDI / attitude-INDI / full INDI)
  implemented as one Rust out-of-tree controller, running identically on hardware and in the
  Crazyswarm2 simulator (same source, via SIL bindings).
- Three physical platforms supported from one switchable config: standard CF2.1,
  thrust-upgraded CF2.1, brushless CF21BL (currently the primary flown platform).
- Residual force measurement (`indi.a_res_* = a_meas − a_model`, the thesis's core signal)
  implemented and logged in every controller mode, not just INDI.
- Two real flight-code bugs found and fixed: position INDI was reinforcing disturbances
  instead of cancelling them (exactly 2.00× on a known test force), and `a_res` was dead under
  the geometric controller, which would have silently blocked half the planned dataset.
- Learned-residual infrastructure built end to end: an onboard deep-sets network (987
  weights), a training pipeline, and a full collect→train→upload→enable→measure dry run —
  proven in simulation, not yet exercised on real data.

**Controller validation, 2026-09-09 → 09-12** (see `docs/FLIGHT_CARD_VALIDATION.md` and
`docs/lab_sessions/`):
- 2026-09-09: six hover flights crashed under both controllers; three real root causes found
  and fixed (a residual sign flip that turned out to destabilize hardware and was parked
  behind a runtime flag; a sample-rate bug in the attitude filters; geometric silently flying
  with the wrong position gains).
- 2026-09-11: re-flew clean (stage 1). Stage 2 (isolating a residual oscillation under
  trajectory tracking) made real progress — bisecting the attitude gains brought peak
  oscillation from 88° down to 7° — but never reached a fully clean pass. A notch-filter
  attempt crashed outright.
- 2026-09-12: ran the card's own H0 diagnostic (attitude-INDI-only vs. position-INDI-only) —
  inconclusive; both sub-loops were individually worse than the combined system, so the two
  loops are too coupled to isolate this way. The notch-filter crash was root-caused offline
  the same day (a missed filter warm-up at the controller handover) and fixed in firmware —
  not yet flown.

**First-ever 2-drone flight, 2026-09-12** (see `docs/lab_sessions/2026-09-12.md`):
- Six real infrastructure bugs found and fixed along the way (gain selection, a controller
  hand-off risk, hover support, arming, logging, a landing collision — all resolved).
- No uSD (on-board) logging or proper plots yet, so no quantitative claim — but the obvious
  observation stands: the lower drone was visibly disturbed by the upper drone's downwash
  while the upper drone stayed steady, exactly the interaction effect the thesis is built to
  measure. A follow-up attempt on our own controller crashed both drones — root-caused and
  fixed (a shared config drift plus a per-robot override bug), not yet re-flown.
