Two Ways to Build INDI

Their INDI and ours turn out to be the same control law, written two different ways. What separates them is conditioning. Chasing that difference turned up a real bug in our own filters — see §08.

01

What is in their firmware

There is no new controller. No new ControllerType, no controller_naindi.c. Everything lives inside the stock controller_lee.c, switched by two PARAM_UINT8 bitmasks. One controller, two orthogonal augmentations.

ParameterBitTurns on
ctrlLee.use_nn&1Network supplies the lateral force residual → a_nn.x, a_nn.y
&2Network supplies the vertical force residual → a_nn.z
&4Network supplies the torque residual → u_nn (outputs 3–5)
ctrlLee.indi&1Force INDI → a_indi
&2Moment INDI → tau_rpm
&4Take the actuator force from PWM instead of RPM — an explicit no-deck path

The four published methods are settings, not programs

Paper labeluse_nnindiWhat it is
Lee00Plain geometric SE(3). The baseline
INDI03Lee + residual rejection measured from RPM. Needs the deck
IL-NDI3–70Lee + learned residual. No RPM at runtime — the paper's selling point
NA-INDI3–73Both. The network predicts, INDI cleans up whatever it missed

Naming, kept straight. The repo and paper say IL-NDI, never “L-INDI”. Our cite key is cobobriesewitz2026lindi. IL-NDI = network only · INDI = RPM only · NA-INDI = both. The network has 6 outputs (3 force, 3 torque) and ~19 inputs including motor PWM ratios — so it substitutes commanded PWM for measured RPM, not nothing at all.

Three bits on indi and three on use_nn give 64 combinations. Most are degenerate. These are the ones that mean something — and the paper reports only the first four.

02

The residual is the same quantity

Before the differences, the thing that makes any comparison legitimate:

Theirs

LMCE/residual_calculation.py

fa = m·aworld − R·fu

Ours

firmware_app/src/lib.rs

ares = ameas − amodel

Identical up to units — fa = m · ares. Both are “measured acceleration minus what the thrust model predicts”. But the physics generating it differs: theirs is a slung payload and aerodynamics on one vehicle; ours is another vehicle's downwash. Only their no-payload numbers are comparable to our single-drone case.

03

They are the same law

worked, not assumed

The instinct is: “theirs is published with a supervisor and a PhD student, so if ours differs, ours must be wrong.” The algebra says otherwise. Start from the rigid body and reduce both.

Rigid body

J·ω̇ = τ_motors + τ_dist − ω×Jω

Ours — substitute τ_current = τ_motors (from RPM) and α_meas = J⁻¹(τ_motors + τ_dist − ω×Jω)

τ_cmd = τ_current + J·(α_ref − α_meas) = τ_motors + J·α_ref − (τ_motors + τ_dist − ω×Jω) = J·α_ref + ω×Jω − τ_dist

Theirs — note τ_imu = J·α − (Jω×ω) = J·α + ω×Jω, which is τ_motors + τ_dist

τ_res = τ_imu − τ_rpm = τ_dist u = u_geo − τ_res = J·α_ref + ω×Jω − τ_dist
τ_cmd = J·α_ref + ω×Jω − τ_dist   — identical

Ours cancels the gyroscopic term implicitly (it is already inside α_meas); theirs handles it explicitly on both sides. This is the known INDI ↔ disturbance-observer correspondence. Ours is faithful to Tal & Karaman's u = u₀ + G⁻¹(ν − α_meas); theirs is the DOB arrangement of the same law.

So neither is wrong. The equivalence is exact for unfiltered signals. Everything that actually separates them appears once filters, clamps and finite sample rates are introduced — which is what the next section enumerates.

04

The two arrangements, in plain terms

same law, two framings

Having established they reduce to the same expression, here is why they nonetheless look like different algorithms — each is written as if answering a different question.

Ours

“What torque am I producing right now? Adjust from there.”

τnew = τcurrent + J·(αwanted − αmeasured)

It reads the torque it is currently producing, from RPM, then adds a correction on top. Because α_meas already contains the disturbance, that correction implicitly cancels it — which is why this reduces to the same expression.

Like steering by “turn the wheel a bit more than it is now.” You must know where the wheel actually is — and if that belief is wrong or late, the error accumulates.

Theirs

“What should the torque be? Then subtract what I can't explain.”

u = ugeometric − (τmeasured − τpredicted)

It computes the ordinary geometric torque, then subtracts the mismatch between measured and modelled torque. Fresh every cycle, nothing remembered.

Like “point the wheel at the target, then correct for the crosswind you just measured.” A bad estimate gives a bad correction once — it cannot pile up.

Three things follow from that

  1. Where the subtraction happens

    Ours differences angular accelerations and multiplies the result by J. Theirs differences torques, having multiplied each side by J first. Algebraically the same — but ours puts all of the J error onto the residual. Our J is a simulator default for a 45 g drone, running on a 41 g brushless, so that is not academic.

  2. What “turning INDI on” actually does

    Ours replaces the entire inner loop and switches to different gains in different units — kr=2400 [1/s²] instead of kr_geo=0.010 [Nm]. Theirs changes nothing: the geometric law runs untouched with the same KR=0.007, and one correction term is subtracted from its output.

  3. Conditioning

    Ours differences raw signals. Theirs clamps and low-pass filters both sides before subtracting — 80 Hz on force, 40 Hz on torque. Our residual reaches the commanded attitude unfiltered.

The bottom line. Theirs is the conservative arrangement: keep the working controller, subtract a measured error. Ours is the incremental one: rebuild the torque command from the actuator state each cycle. But as the next section shows, these are the same law written two ways — the real differences are in conditioning, not in the method.

05

The two arrangements, formally

two arrangements

The same two framings as equations. Both compute J·α_ref + ω×Jω − τ_dist — they differ in which quantities are named and filtered on the way there, not in what they produce.

Ours Incremental

Tal & Karaman. Differences angular accelerations.

αref = αdes − kR·eR − kω·eω
Δτ   = J·(αref,f − αmeas)
τ    = τcurrent + Δτ
τcurrent from RPM

Gains kr=2400, kw=170 in [1/s²] — an angular acceleration, only becoming torque via J.

Theirs Subtractive

Disturbance-observer form. Differences torques.

τres = τimu,f − τrpm,f
u    = ugeo − τres − unn

τrpm from RPM

Gains KR={0.007,0.007,0.01}, Kω={0.002,…} in [Nm] — the same unit system as our kr_geo/kw_geo.

Ours can close a loop through its own previous output — but only as a fallback. At the shipped defaults it reads τ_current from RPM, exactly as theirs does.

A correction worth stating plainly

An earlier version of this page argued that ours carries actuator memory (tau_prev, tau_act) which can limit-cycle, while theirs has none. That was overstated. At our shipped defaults — act_tau = 0 with RPM present — τ_current comes straight from RPM, exactly as their τ_rpm does. The memory path is a fallback, used only when RPM is unavailable. We also already phase-match via filt_tau = 1.

So the actuator-memory story is not the explanation for the 5–8 Hz shake. What remains genuinely different is where the subtraction happens, how each side is filtered and clamped, and the gyro and dt sources — all enumerated next.

The observation that reframes the rest

“Conditioning” is not one thing. Our attitude INDI is carefully conditioned; our position INDI is not conditioned at all.

Torque side (ours): α_ref, α_meas and τ_current are all Butterworth-filtered at 60 Hz and phase-matched via filt_tau=1. Genuinely careful work.

Force side (ours): a_res = a_meas − a_model, raw. No filter, no clamp, straight into f_d → thrust_vec → desired_rot() → R_d → the commanded attitude.

The 09-09 crash was at ctrl_mode=3, which enables the position loop. The unconditioned path is exactly the one that was live when it diverged.

07

Do both need the RPM deck?

By default, yes — both compute the modelled force from measured RPM. But the two are not equally dependent on it.

PathForce sourceNeeds RPM deck?
Their INDI, defaultmeasured RPM → κf·rpm²yes
Their INDI, indi&4PWM → inverted calibration → κf·rpm²no — synthetic RPM
Their network (IL-NDI)motorsGetRatio() — PWM onlynever
Ours, all INDI modesrpm_get_all() onlyyes — no fallback

The asymmetry that matters. Their INDI can fall back to commanded PWM by inverting a linear rpm ↔ pwm calibration. Ours cannot: rpm_get_all() is the only source, and without it a_res reads exactly zero and INDI silently degrades to geometric. That is already flagged as an abort condition on our flight card — but it is worth knowing a published contingency exists if the deck ever fails mid-campaign.

And the whole point of IL-NDI is that the network never touches RPM at all. It substitutes commanded PWM for measured RPM — not nothing. That is a weaker claim than “no actuator information”, and worth stating precisely if we cite it.

⚠️ Oddity: their PWM branch still sits inside if (self->indi && rpm_deck_available) — gated behind the very deck it is meant to replace. Either an oversight, or they always fly with the deck fitted and use PWM mode only for like-for-like comparison.

08

What the investigation found

10 September

Acting on §06. Everything below is behind runtime parameters that default to today's behaviour — verified, not assumed: the new build is 0/100 samples different from the previous one across all four ctrl_modes. The validation flights are unaffected.

🔴 Our filters have been designed for the wrong sample rate — all along

controllerOutOfTree is called from stabilizer.c's main loop unconditionally at RATE_MAIN_LOOP = 1000 Hz — “the sensor should unlock at 1kHz”. There is no RATE_DO_EXECUTE(ATTITUDE_RATE) gate, contrary to what firmware_app/CLAUDE.md claimed. Our dt computation is correct (0.001). Only the filter coefficients were hard-coded to DT = 0.002.

The parameter saysWhat you actually get
fc_bw = 60 Hz−3 dB at ~206 Hz — 3.4× less filtering
notch_f0 = 6.9 Hzcentred at 13.8 Hz — exactly 2×

The notch result is the striking one. The stage-2 notch aimed at the measured 6.9 Hz peak was notching 13.8 Hz and never touched it. That is a concrete explanation for “notch filter tested + failed”, and it plausibly explains why the fc_bw sweeps read as flat — the swept range never reached the shake band.

This has been true for every flight, including the successful July campaign, so it is not the crash cause — the gains were tuned empirically around it. But fc_bw and notch_f0 do not mean what they say, which matters for every future filter decision.

⚠️ Correcting it is not a free fix

A real 60 Hz filter lags more than a 206 Hz one. Extra phase lag if filt_dt_us is corrected to 1000:

fshipped (~206 Hz)corrected (60 Hz)extra lag
5.0 Hz−1.69°−3.38°−1.69°
6.9 Hz−2.33°−4.66°−2.33°
20 Hz−6.78°−13.65°−6.87°

The shake is a phase problem, so correcting the sample rate could plausibly make it worse. Treat it as a deliberate single-variable A/B — and note that fc_bw = 120 reproduces today's coefficients exactly at the corrected rate, if you want the parameter honest without changing the behaviour.

🔴 Result, 11 September: correcting the sample rate is confirmed a real no-op — and then corrected fc_bw down made things worse

The predicted no-op point was actually 206 Hz, not 120 — flown and confirmed byte-identical to the shipped behaviour (hover std 1.13° vs 2a's 1.12°, shake on circle/figure8 unchanged in magnitude). Good validation that the corrected-rate math here was right.

Lowering it from there (fc_bw=100) is exactly the "not a free fix" this call-out warned about, and it played out that way: hover regressed from clean to a growing 17.3° peak, and circle's peak roll nearly tripled (27.5°→69.2°). Reverted. This system's real operating point needs looser filtering, not tighter — consistent with the extra-phase-lag table above, not a contradiction of it.

⚪ Negative result — the gyro-source hypothesis is not promising

Our sensors->gyro does carry a stock 2nd-order 80 Hz LPF (GYRO_LPF_CUTOFF_FREQ 80) in series with our own, where they take raw gyroNoLpf. But the total phase lag at 6.9 Hz is only −5.83° (−3.50° stock, −2.33° ours) — too small to drive the oscillation. Recorded as ruled out; the effective cutoff error above is the far larger effect on the same path.

09

Recommendations

my view — decide for yourself

Ordered by value-per-risk. Nothing here should be done before stage 1 of the validation flights — changing the controller now would make tomorrow's result uninterpretable.

1 · Condition a_res before it enters the loop

✓ flown 11 September — clean, no regression

indi_gains.res_fc=80, indi_gains.res_clamp=10 flown for the first time: INDI hover PASS, matching the stage-1 baseline exactly. No effect either way on the trajectory-tracking shake (expected — this conditions the residual feed, not the attitude loop). Left enabled through the rest of the session.

a_res is a difference of two noisy estimates — accelerometer (noisy) minus RPM² model (quantised, actuator-lagged). Differencing amplifies both. Unfiltered, that noise becomes a commanded attitude every tick.

Filter both sides identically, then subtract — that way the filter lag cancels in the difference, which filtering after the subtraction cannot achieve. Add a norm clamp so one bad sample cannot reach the law at all. Their numbers (10 m/s², 80 Hz) are a starting point, not gospel — our airframe differs.

Why first: it is the one place where they do something and we do literally nothing, on exactly the path that was live during the crash. Contained change, no effect on the method's identity.

2 · dt resolution

✓ flown 11 September — confirmed genuine no-op at fc_bw=206

Not the quantisation I expected — something bigger. The loop runs at 1000 Hz and our dt is correct; the filter coefficients were wrong. See §08. filt_dt_us=1000 + filt_prewarp=1 + fc_bw=206 flown and confirmed byte-identical in behaviour to the old 60 Hz/legacy-timing config (hover 1.13° vs 1.12° std). Tuning fc_bw down from there made things worse, not better — see the call-out above.

α_raw = Δω / dt, so any error in dt scales α_meas directly. Ours is (tick − last_tick) × 0.001 — millisecond resolution. If the scheduler is exact (tick always +2) this is fine. Any jitter quantises to 1 ms steps, i.e. up to 50 % error in that sample's angular acceleration. Theirs uses usecTimestamp() and cannot do this.

Cheap first step: log dt for one flight and look at its distribution. If it is always exactly 0.002, close the question permanently. Note the precedent — “2 kHz call with ms tick” was one of the five sim-fidelity bugs.

3 · Reconsider the gyro source

○ investigated — ruled out

The stock 80 Hz LPF is in series with ours, but total phase lag at 6.9 Hz is only −5.83° — too small to drive the oscillation. See §08.

They take the raw gyro and filter downstream, after converting to torque. We take the stock-LPF'd gyro and filter again — an unknown filter in series with ours, so the true phase response is hard to reason about.

Why it matters here: the 5–8 Hz shake is a phase problem, and four investigations have not closed it. Having two filters in series where one was intended is exactly the kind of thing that survives four investigations.

4 · Per-axis filtering for yaw

⏸ deferred

Yaw torque comes from motor drag-torque differences (t2t ≈ 0.006) — an order of magnitude smaller than roll/pitch arm torque, and correspondingly noisier. They filter it 4× harder on purpose. We treat all three axes identically.

Lower priority: our shake is roll/pitch, so this is not the cause. It is a considered choice we have simply not made.

5 · Do not switch to their subtractive form

keep ours

It is algebraically the same law, so it would buy no correctness — and it would change what “INDI” denotes in the comparison. Tal & Karaman incremental INDI is the method under study.

If it is ever wanted: add it as a second INDI variant to the comparison, honestly labelled, rather than replacing ours.

Sequencing, and one honest caveat

Nothing before the validation flights. Stage 1 exists to establish whether the restored configuration flies; changing the controller first destroys that. All of the above is already in the firmware but switched off — the new build is 0/100 samples different from the previous one at defaults, across all four ctrl_modes.

Order run on 11 September, one variable at a time: uSD cards pulled; res_fc + res_clamp on, flown clean; filt_dt_us=1000 + fc_bw=206, confirmed genuine no-op; fc_bw tuned down, made things worse, reverted; the notch (notch_f0=6.9, the actual corrected frequency) flown for the very first time — and it crashed (stable to 6.4s, then 88.7°/66.6° divergence within 0.7s, altitude collapse, real impact). Root cause not understood; parked, disabled pending offline analysis.

Then the shake-band test — our attitude loop's ωn is 7.80 Hz and the shake is 6.3–7.9 Hz, so kr was dropped 2400→987 (ωn 5.0 Hz, ζ 1.74). Alone, with pos_gains unchanged, it diverged — a real, distinct finding: pos_gains were tuned assuming the original 7.8 Hz attitude bandwidth, so slowing the attitude loop alone breaks the cascade's timescale separation. Re-tried with pos_gains scaled to match: hover passed cleanly (best of the session), but the trajectory-tracking shake was unchanged — still 17.6° peak vs the stiff baseline's 17.7°. Bisecting further to kr=483 (ωn 3.5 Hz) dropped the peak to 11.4° but broke position holding; switching to geometric's own pos_gains alongside it fixed position holding and dropped the peak further to 6.7°, but introduced a small new hover growing trend. No configuration reached a clean pass on both axes together across four attempts — real, substantial progress (88°→7° peak), not a resolution. Ended reverted to the confirmed-clean locked point. Full account: docs/lab_sessions/2026-09-11.md.

A sim attempt to de-risk the kr/pos_gains coupling before flying it live failed to produce a usable answer: the sim's idealized actuators and near-perfect position estimate didn't reproduce the hardware divergence at all, even at the exact failed gains. Not trustworthy for this specific question without a higher-fidelity plant.

The caveat stands, sharpened by results: everything in §08–09 was reasoning from their code, not a controlled experiment on our airframe, and that's exactly what showed up — the no-op prediction held, but the "tune fc_bw down" and "notch will finally work" predictions didn't survive contact with this airframe. Their platform is a standard CF2.1 (J ≈ 16.6·10⁻⁶, ~30 g), their disturbance is a slung payload, their gains don't transfer. Next: the card's own H0 partition (ctrl_mode=2 then 1) to isolate which INDI sub-loop actually carries the shake — never run yet, and the natural next step after four gain attempts on full INDI converged on the same result.
