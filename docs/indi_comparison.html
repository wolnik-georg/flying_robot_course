Two Ways to Build INDI

What the NA-INDI authors actually implemented, how it differs from ours, and why those differences bear directly on the two problems currently blocking the lab track.

01

What is in their firmware

There is no new controller. No new ControllerType, no controller_naindi.c. Everything lives inside the stock controller_lee.c, switched by two PARAM_UINT8 bitmasks. One controller, two orthogonal augmentations.

ParameterBitTurns on
ctrlLee.use_nn&1Network supplies the lateral force residual → a_nn.x, a_nn.y
&2Network supplies the vertical force residual → a_nn.z
&4Network supplies the torque residual → u_nn (outputs 3–5)
ctrlLee.indi&1Force INDI → a_indi
&2Moment INDI → tau_rpm

The four published methods are settings, not programs

Paper labeluse_nnindiWhat it is
Lee00Plain geometric SE(3). The baseline
INDI03Lee + residual rejection measured from RPM. Needs the deck
IL-NDI3–70Lee + learned residual. No RPM at runtime — the paper's selling point
NA-INDI3–73Both. The network predicts, INDI cleans up whatever it missed

Naming, kept straight. The repo and paper say IL-NDI, never “L-INDI”. Our cite key is cobobriesewitz2026lindi. IL-NDI = network only · INDI = RPM only · NA-INDI = both. The network has 6 outputs (3 force, 3 torque) and ~19 inputs including motor PWM ratios — so it substitutes commanded PWM for measured RPM, not nothing at all.

02

The residual is the same quantity

Before the differences, the thing that makes any comparison legitimate:

Theirs

LMCE/residual_calculation.py

fa = m·aworld − R·fu

Ours

firmware_app/src/lib.rs

ares = ameas − amodel

Identical up to units — fa = m · ares. Both are “measured acceleration minus what the thrust model predicts”. But the physics generating it differs: theirs is a slung payload and aerodynamics on one vehicle; ours is another vehicle's downwash. Only their no-payload numbers are comparable to our single-drone case.

03

Two different formulations

bears on the oscillation

This is the structural heart of it. Both reject an unmodelled torque, but they are not the same algorithm.

Ours Incremental

Tal & Karaman. Builds on the previous actuator state.

αref = αdes − kR·eR − kω·eω
Δτ   = J·(αref,f − αmeas)
τ    = τcurrent + Δτ
τprev ← τ   ← memory

Gains kr=2400, kw=170 in [1/s²] — an angular acceleration, only becoming torque via J.

Theirs Subtractive

A residual term bolted onto an unchanged geometric law.

τres = τimu,f − τrpm,f
u    = ugeo − τres − unn

no memory, no increment

Gains KR={0.007,0.007,0.01}, Kω={0.002,…} in [Nm] — the same unit system as our kr_geo/kw_geo.

The difference in one picture: ours closes a loop through its own previous output; theirs does not.

Why this bears on the 5–8 Hz brushless shake

An incremental law carries internal state — tau_prev, tau_act, and an assumed actuator lag. When that assumed lag mismatches the real one, the loop has its own dynamics and can limit-cycle. That is precisely the standing hypothesis in investigation_indi_oscillation_2026-07-21.md, where τ = 44 ms was measured against 71 ms and never resolved.

Their formulation has no such state and structurally cannot do it. Enabling their INDI does not change a single attitude gain — the geometric law runs untouched and a residual is subtracted from its output. It is a strictly more conservative route to the same goal, and it is published and flying.

05

What it suggests

For the crash — the sign was probably never the problem

Their published, flying law uses the same sign that crashed us (Fd = ad − aindi − ann). What differs is conditioning: they clamp and 80 Hz-filter both sides before differencing. Ours is a raw instantaneous difference that goes straight into f_d → Rd → the commanded attitude.

So the fix is likely filter and clamp a_res, not abandon the sign. That becomes the leading candidate once the validation flights pass — it is not a reason to change anything before them.

For the oscillation — a published alternative exists

Adopting their subtractive form would remove the actuator-memory dynamics entirely. But this is a thesis decision, not just an implementation one: it changes what “INDI” denotes in our comparison, and our INDI is deliberately Tal & Karaman because that is the method under study.

The honest framing: their form is a legitimate second INDI variant that could be added to the comparison, not a drop-in repair for ours.

Caveats — read before acting

Their platform is a standard CF2.1 (J ≈ 16.6·10⁻⁶ kg·m², ~30 g), not our 41 g brushless, so gains and lag constants do not transfer. Their disturbance is a slung payload, not downwash. And their tau filter init loop runs i<2 where the acc loop runs i<3 — their z-axis torque filter is never initialised. Looks like a typo; do not copy it uncritically.
