\section{Limitations \& Complications}

\begin{frame}{Limitations --- The Brushless ``Shaky Shaky''}
\begin{block}{What we saw}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Full INDI on brushless CF21BL: a self-sustained \textasciitilde7\,Hz oscillation.
  \item Reproducible in hover \emph{and} under maneuvering load --- same mechanism throughout.
  \item \textbf{This is why brushless underperforms upgraded on some maneuvers} (e.g.\
        crash ceiling on oval/circle at high kt) despite otherwise excellent figure-8 tracking ---
        an unexpected, counterintuitive combination worth flagging explicitly.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Root Cause, Quantified --- And Why the Obvious Fixes Don't Work}
\centering
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.1}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Factor & Measured & Verdict \\
\midrule
Resonance \(\omega_n=\sqrt{k_r}/2\pi\) & 7.8\,Hz (kr=2400) vs.\ 7.22\,Hz shake & \textbf{root cause} \\
Actuator lag @ 7.22\,Hz & 32\,ms $\rightarrow$ \textbf{82\textdegree} phase loss & \textbf{dominant} \\
EKF attitude lag @ 7.22\,Hz & 7.9\,ms $\rightarrow$ 20\textdegree\ phase loss & minor \\
\midrule
Filter (fc\_bw=70\,Hz) & alp\_filt/alp\_raw ratio = 1.00 & dead end \\
Detuning kr ($\approx$600) & still shaky; vibration is broadband & dead end \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\vspace{0.1cm}

\begin{tiny}
\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0.03cm}
\item \textbf{Resonance}: $k_r$ sets the attitude loop's natural frequency ($\omega_n=\sqrt{k_r}/2\pi$). At $k_r=2400$ that's 7.8\,Hz --- almost exactly the 7.22\,Hz shake, i.e. the controller is oscillating near its \emph{own} natural frequency, not reacting to external vibration.
\item \textbf{Actuator lag}: motor/ESC/prop response takes 32\,ms to catch up to a commanded change; at 7.22\,Hz that's 82\textdegree\ of phase shift --- enough to turn a stabilizing correction into a destabilizing one one cycle later. Confirmed in-flight under real 4-motor load, not bench-only --- why it's the dominant term.
\item \textbf{EKF lag}: the attitude \emph{estimate} lags true motion by only 7.9\,ms (20\textdegree\ phase) --- an order of magnitude smaller than actuator lag, ruled out as the main driver.
\item \textbf{Filter}: exists specifically to remove noise like this, but at fc\_bw=70\,Hz it passes 7.22\,Hz completely unchanged. Cutting the cutoff low enough to actually attenuate 7.22\,Hz means filtering right at that frequency --- reintroducing the same actuator-lag phase-loss mechanism above, right where correction authority is needed most. Same failure mode in a different place, not a fix.
\item \textbf{Detuning $k_r$}: moves $\omega_n$ away from 7.22\,Hz, but still shaky at $k_r\approx600$ --- the underlying vibration is broadband, not one clean tone, so there's no quiet $k_r$ to retune into without giving up tracking bandwidth.
\end{itemize}
\end{tiny}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Real Fix Candidates --- Correctly Not Pursued}
\begin{small}
\begin{itemize}
  \setlength{\itemsep}{0.1cm}
  \item \textbf{Primary candidate --- most consistent with the data}: a \textbf{narrow band-reject
        (notch) filter around 5--10\,Hz}, instead of a broad low-pass. The shake is a genuine,
        narrow spectral peak at 7.22\,Hz (hover \emph{and} maneuvering load alike) --- not
        broadband noise. A plain low-pass cut low enough to touch it reintroduces the same
        phase-lag mechanism as the actuator lag itself (Root Cause slide); a notch targeting just
        that band removes the resonance while leaving the rest of the spectrum --- and its phase ---
        untouched. The actual point of filtering, done narrowly instead of broadly.
  \item Reduce actuator lag directly: faster ESC/motor/prop response, or a delay-compensating
        term in the INDI control law.
  \item Reduce vibration at the source: prop balancing, mount damping.
  \item \textbf{Speculative, unproven}: motor-protocol-level rate/current limiting (e.g.\ DShot
        slew-rate limiting) to blunt the torque swings feeding the resonance --- not yet backed by
        data collected this project.
\end{itemize}
\end{small}
{\footnotesize\textbf{All require hardware/firmware iteration beyond this project's remaining
time} --- a scope decision, not a gap: diagnosis complete and quantified, fix is future work.}
\end{frame}

\section{Summary}

\begin{frame}{Summary}
\begin{small}
\begin{enumerate}
  \setlength{\itemsep}{0.2cm}
  \item \textbf{Motion planning}: built a pipeline that generates optimal trajectories for the
        drone --- the foundation everything else in this project flies on.
  \item \textbf{Baseline}: established and tuned a geometric controller on the standard CF2.1 for
        optimal tracking error, as the reference every other mode is measured against.
  \item \textbf{INDI}: implemented and deployed full INDI on \textbf{three platforms} ---
        standard, thrust-upgraded, and brushless CF21BL.
  \item \textbf{Comparison}: fair, apples-to-apples evaluation across all four modes --- INDI
        beats geometric on every platform, and brushless beats standard/upgraded on raw tracking
        (best figure-8 RMSE of all four, 2.45\,cm).
  \item \textbf{Artifacts}: flights across the full trajectory library (figure-8, oval, circle,
        slalom, ...) on multiple platforms, at multiple speeds --- the concrete outcome of the
        project.
  \item \textbf{Limitations}: brushless "shaky shaky" behavior (diagnosed, not fixed) and the
        teardrop/inverted-loop maneuver (not attempted) --- both correctly scoped out given
        remaining project time.
\end{enumerate}
\end{small}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Appendix --- Motion Planning, In Brief}
\begin{small}
\begin{enumerate}
  \setlength{\itemsep}{0.25cm}
  \item \textbf{Waypoints in, smooth path out}: a handful of position (+ yaw) waypoints go in;
        a piecewise polynomial trajectory that passes through all of them comes out.
  \item \textbf{Minimum-snap polynomials}: each segment between waypoints is a degree-7/8
        polynomial, solved so the whole path is as smooth as possible (continuous up to snap,
        the 4th derivative of position) --- this is what keeps the drone from having to make any
        sudden, jerky corrections.
  \item \textbf{Segment timing, two ways}: either give each segment a duration by hand (Mode 0,
        Mellinger 2012), or give one aggressiveness scalar $k_t$ and let the planner pick
        timings automatically (Mode 1, Richter et al.\ 2016) --- this is the $k_t$ used
        throughout every sweep in this talk.
  \item \textbf{Differential flatness turns a path into a full reference}: position and its
        derivatives (velocity, acceleration, jerk, snap) plus yaw are enough to solve, in closed
        form, for everything the controller needs --- desired attitude $R_d$, thrust, body rate
        $\omega_d$, and angular acceleration $\dot\omega_d$ (Faessler, Franchi \& Scaramuzza 2018).
        No attitude has to be authored by hand.
  \item \textbf{Result}: a complete, physically consistent feedforward reference at every instant
        in time --- not just a position setpoint --- exported as polynomial coefficients and
        evaluated onboard at 500--1000\,Hz. Both the geometric and INDI controllers consume the
        exact same reference.
\end{enumerate}
\end{small}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Appendix --- Full INDI, Pseudocode}
\framesubtitle{Simplified from \texttt{firmware\_app/src/lib.rs} \texttt{controller\_step()} --- filtering/clamping omitted for clarity}
\ttfamily\scriptsize
\textbf{every control cycle (dt):}\\[2pt]
\textit{-- position loop (outer, full rate) --}\\
e\_p = p\_d - p;\quad e\_v = v\_d - v\\
a\_model = ($\sum_i k_{t,i}$rpm$_i^2$)/mass $\cdot\, R\,e_z - g\,e_z$\quad\textit{\small(predicted accel, from RPM\textsuperscript{2} thrust model)}\\
a\_meas~ = $R\cdot$accel\_body $- g\,e_z$\quad\textit{\small(measured accel, from IMU)}\\
\textbf{a\_indi = a\_meas -- a\_model}\quad\textit{\small $\leftarrow$ INDI increment: what the model misses}\\
f\_d = a\_d + Kp$\,$e\_p + Kv$\,$e\_v + Ki$\int$e\_p + $g\,e_z$ + a\_indi\\
$R_d,\ \omega_d,\ \dot\omega_{des}$ = flatness(f\_d$\cdot$mass, yaw\_d)\quad\textit{\small(desired attitude, from planning)}\\[3pt]
\textit{-- attitude loop (inner), the "full INDI" part --}\\
e\_R = 0.5$\,$vee($R_d^\top R - R^\top R_d$)\quad\textit{\small(attitude error on SO(3))}\\
e$_\omega$ = $\omega - \omega_d$\\
$\alpha$\_ref = $\dot\omega_{des} - K_r\,$e\_R $- K_w\,$e$_\omega$\quad\textit{\small(desired angular accel, Tal \& Karaman Eq.\,28)}\\
$\alpha$\_meas = lowpass(diff($\omega$))\quad\textit{\small(measured angular accel, from gyro)}\\
$\tau$\_current = rpm\_to\_torque(m1..m4)\quad\textit{\small(current torque, from motor RPM --- not a model)}\\
\textbf{$\Delta\tau$ = J$\,(\alpha$\_ref -- $\alpha$\_meas)}\quad\textit{\small $\leftarrow$ INDI increment: correct only the error}\\
$\tau$ = $\tau$\_current + $\Delta\tau$\\[3pt]
send(thrust, $\tau$) $\to$ motor mixer
\normalfont
\vspace{0.2cm}

\begin{footnotesize}
\textbf{The core idea}: instead of computing torque from a dynamics model alone, INDI takes the
\emph{currently measured} torque/acceleration (from RPM and gyro) as the baseline and computes
only a small correction for the gap between desired and measured --- far less sensitive to model
error (motor dynamics, drag, mass) than a purely model-based controller.
\end{footnotesize}
